Export websites and Google SEO for Zhejiang manufacturers

Zhejiang is China’s private-enterprise province: more than four-fifths of its trade is handled by private firms, from Ningbo’s component makers to Yiwu’s traders and Taizhou’s pump and car factories. This guide summarises the province’s 2025 official figures and profiles its manufacturing cities.

Zhejiang’s manufacturing economy in official figures

The Zhejiang Provincial Bureau of Statistics’ 2025 bulletin gives the province-wide picture below.

  • Zhejiang’s GDP was 9,454.5 billion yuan in 2025, up 5.5%, and value added of industry above designated size grew 6.9%.[1]
  • Among the main industries, automobiles grew 19.7%, computers, communications and electronics 16.5%, chemical raw materials 7.0%, general-purpose equipment 6.9% and electrical machinery 6.1%.[1]
  • Equipment manufacturing made up 52.7% of above-designated-size value added and high-tech manufacturing 18.2%; new-energy vehicle output was 1.38 million (+49.8%).[1]
  • Goods trade was 5,546.1 billion yuan (+5.4%) and exports 4,184.9 billion yuan (+7.2%); private firms handled 82.2% of trade, with 250 countries and regions as partners.[1]
  • Trade with ASEAN grew 16.4%, with Africa 11.5% and with the EU 8.7%; mechanical and electrical products were 47.0% of exports.[1]

Zhejiang’s exporters are mostly private, specialised and sell directly. Their websites are often their main sales tool abroad — and the difference between a generic template and a specific, well-structured site shows up directly in the inquiries they get.

Website and search priorities for Zhejiang exporters

  1. Lead with your specialism: one clear product family on the first screen, not a list of everything.
  2. Use model tables with ratings, sizes and materials so buyers can compare quickly.
  3. Put certificates on the product pages they cover, with the market each applies to.
  4. Separate OEM/private-label pages from own-brand products.
  5. Add language versions for growth markets — ASEAN, Latin America, Africa — only if you will maintain them.
  6. Measure which product families bring RFQs, and expand those.
