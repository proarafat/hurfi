Export websites and Google SEO for Liaoning manufacturers

Liaoning is northeast China’s industrial heartland: ships and petrochemicals in Dalian, equipment in Shenyang, steel and magnesite in Anshan, steel, aluminium and port trade in Yingkou. Its exports grew strongly in 2025 even as industry was flat. This guide summarises the province’s official figures and profiles its manufacturing cities.

Liaoning’s manufacturing economy in official figures

The Liaoning Provincial Bureau of Statistics’ 2025 bulletin gives the province-wide picture below.

  • Liaoning’s GDP was 3,318.29 billion yuan in 2025, up 3.7%; value added of industry above designated size grew 0.6%, with high-tech manufacturing up 2.6% and equipment manufacturing down 0.8%.[1]
  • Goods trade was 748.56 billion yuan (−2.0%), but exports rose 8.0% to 407.73 billion yuan; private firms’ exports grew 20.7%.[1]
  • Exports to Belt and Road countries rose 23.7% to 209.37 billion yuan, and to ASEAN 37.8% to 57.44 billion yuan.[1]
  • Mechanical and electrical products were 206.36 billion yuan of exports (+8.0%) and food products 32.42 billion yuan (+9.8%).[1]
  • Industrial export delivery value was 297.06 billion yuan (+2.2%), and ports handled 13.682 million TEU of containers (+1.5%).[1]

Liaoning’s growth in 2025 came from exports — especially private firms selling to Belt and Road and ASEAN markets. Those buyers often find suppliers online, so a clear English website with specifications and shipping details has become part of the sales process.

More Liaoning manufacturing cities, from their 2025 bulletins

These cities do not have a full guide yet. The figures come from each city’s 2025 statistical bulletin, listed in the sources below.

Anshan

  • Anshan’s GDP was 218.04 billion yuan in 2025 (+5.5%), and value added of industry above designated size grew 9.3%.[2]
  • Iron ore grew 12.7%, steel and deep processing 6.8% and the magnesite industry 14.1%; output of automation instruments rose 42.1% to 975,000 units.[2]
  • Industrial export delivery value was 9.98 billion yuan (+1.5%), including 6.13 billion yuan from steel and 1.34 billion yuan from magnesite (+15.9%); exports were 17.83 billion yuan (−5.2%).[2]

Anshan’s steel, magnesite and refractory suppliers sell on grade, composition and standards. Publishing those per product — with test certificates — is the most useful thing their websites can do. Related: Mining & Mineral Processing Equipment · Anshan in the China directory.

Yingkou

  • Yingkou’s GDP was 157.02 billion yuan in 2025 (+4.0%); value added of industry above designated size grew 9.8%, with high-tech manufacturing up 10.0%.[3]
  • Steel and deep processing made up 41.5% of above-designated-size value added (+17.7%), petrochemicals 17.3% (+4.6%), aluminium 10.6% (+31.7%) and equipment 10.2% (+3.3%).[3]
  • Exports were 24.28 billion yuan (+1.4%); the port handled 220 million tonnes of cargo and 5.846 million TEU of containers.[3]

Yingkou is a port city with steel, aluminium and petrochemical industries. Suppliers there should combine grade and specification pages with a strong shipping page — the port is one of their advantages. Related: Chemicals & Specialty Materials · Yingkou in the China directory.

Website and search priorities for Liaoning exporters

  1. Lead with specifications and standards for every grade, alloy or machine.
  2. Publish test certificates and approvals, with scope and market.
  3. Explain shipping from Dalian, Yingkou or Dandong, with lead times.
  4. Add after-sales pages for equipment: installation, parts and remote support.
  5. Plan Russian, Korean or Japanese pages only where you sell.
  6. Measure inquiries by product and country.
