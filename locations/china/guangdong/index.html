Guangdong’s manufacturing economy in official figures

The Guangdong Provincial Bureau of Statistics’ 2025 bulletin gives the province-wide picture below.

  • Guangdong’s GDP was 14,584.676 billion yuan in 2025, up 3.9%; value added of industry above designated size grew 3.0%.[1]
  • Equipment manufacturing made up 59.6% of above-designated-size industrial value added, advanced manufacturing 56.2% and high-tech manufacturing 34.7%.[1]
  • The province made 1.1829 million new-energy vehicles (+27.7%), 336,300 industrial robot sets (+31.2%), 7.6136 million civilian drones (+39.0%) and 2.9461 million servers (+28.2%).[1]
  • Goods trade was 9,494.16 billion yuan (+4.4%), 20.9% of China’s total; exports were 6,034.86 billion yuan (+2.5%), of which high-tech products made up 33.6%.[1]
  • Private firms handled 63.9% of trade, and ports handled 80.9704 million TEU of containers (+4.5%).[1]

Guangdong’s exporters face a very crowded comparison: a buyer looking for almost any manufactured product will find dozens of Guangdong suppliers. The ones who win are specific — about what they make, the standards they meet and how they ship — and they make that easy to check online.

More Guangdong manufacturing cities, from their 2025 bulletins

These cities do not have a full guide yet. The figures come from each city’s 2025 statistical bulletin, listed in the sources below.

Jiangmen

  • Jiangmen’s GDP was 429.391 billion yuan in 2025 (+2.8%); its 3,870 industrial firms above designated size grew 3.4%.[2]
  • Equipment manufacturing reached 35.7% of above-designated-size value added (+13.6%): rail, ship, aerospace and other transport equipment grew 31.9%, general-purpose equipment 13.0% and automobiles 8.6%.[2]
  • Traditional industries fell 4.8%, including household appliances (−1.1%), furniture (−4.1%) and textiles and clothing (−5.9%). Exports rose 5.0% to 169.17 billion yuan, and trade with Belt and Road countries 8.7%.[2]

Jiangmen’s growth is in equipment, while its appliance, furniture and textile makers are under pressure. For both groups, product pages with real specifications and market-specific certifications are the starting point. Related: Marine & Shipbuilding Supply Chain · Jiangmen in the China directory.

Website and search priorities for Guangdong exporters

  1. Be specific on the first screen: what you make, for which industry and to which standards. Generic “manufacturer and trader” headlines are lost among Guangdong’s many suppliers.
  2. Give each product line its own page with specifications as text and certificates per market.
  3. Explain shipping from your nearest port — Shenzhen, Guangzhou, Zhuhai or others — with lead times and terms.
  4. If you exhibit at the Canton Fair or other fairs, make the website match what buyers saw at the booth.
  5. Plan language versions only for markets you really sell to, and tag them correctly.
  6. Measure inquiries by product line and country from day one.
