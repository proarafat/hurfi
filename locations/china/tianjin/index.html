What Tianjin makes, according to official data

The figures below are from Tianjin’s 2025 statistical bulletin, published on the Tianjin municipal government portal.

  • Industrial value added was 571.87 billion yuan in 2025, up 4.2%; manufacturing grew 4.9%.[1]
  • The twelve key industrial chains made up 81.9% of above-designated-size industry. Connected vehicles grew 25.3%, IT application innovation 9.9%, aerospace 9.7%, biomedicine 9.6% and new materials 9.3%.[1]
  • High-tech manufacturing grew 5.3% and made up 16.6%. Output of industrial robots rose 17.9%, service robots 6.4% and optical fibre 24.8%.[1]
  • Exports were 430.94 billion yuan, up 10.1%. Mechanical and electrical exports were 287.11 billion yuan, up 10.3%, and exports of medicines and medicinal materials 19.71 billion yuan, up 69.7%.[1]
  • Tianjin Port handled 24.03 million TEU of containers, up 3.2%, and 595 million tonnes of cargo, with 150 container routes.[1]

Tianjin buyers are often large integrators, vehicle makers, aerospace programmes or pharma companies with strict supplier processes. A Tianjin supplier’s website should show where it sits in the chain — raw material, component, subsystem or finished product — and the quality approvals that go with it.

Tianjin’s main industries and what buyers compare

The clusters below follow the official figures above. Each links to our page for that industry.

Aerospace

Aerospace buyers check approvals, traceability, special processes and the programmes you can mention.

Connected and electric vehicles

Vehicle suppliers need component pages with test standards, software or connectivity details and quality systems.

Biomedicine

Pharma and biotech suppliers must state GMP status, registrations and documents per market precisely.

New materials

Materials buyers search by grade, property and standard; publish a data page per grade.

Robotics and automation

Robot and automation suppliers should explain payload, reach, controllers, integration and service.

Industrial zones and districts in Tianjin

  • The Tianjin Economic-Technological Development Area (TEDA), established in 1984, has a planned area of 403 square kilometres and describes itself as a demonstration base for electronics, automotive, petrochemicals and biopharmaceuticals.[4]
  • By the end of 2023 TEDA hosted 573 projects from 124 Fortune Global 500 companies, including FAW Toyota, FAW-Volkswagen, Samsung Mobile Display and Novo Nordisk; foreign-invested firms produced over 70% of its industrial output.[4]

TEDA, in Binhai New Area near the port, is where many of Tianjin’s automotive, electronics and pharma suppliers work alongside multinational customers. Supplier websites there are judged against international supplier standards.

Ports, logistics and trade fairs

  • In 2025 Tianjin Port added new direct container routes to the US West Coast, the Black Sea and India.[1]
  • Tianjin held 118 exhibitions and events in 2025, including an international mining conference, an international shipping industry expo and an international helicopter expo.[1]

Buyers comparing suppliers ask how goods will reach them, how long it takes and on what trade terms. Shipping ports, typical lead times and Incoterms on your website answer that before it is asked.

Export profile, typical products and buyer markets

  • Exports to Belt and Road partner countries grew 13.8%; general-trade exports were 243.72 billion yuan (+2.9%) and processing-trade exports 122.93 billion yuan (+16.5%).[1]

Tianjin’s exports grew fastest in medicines and processing trade, and towards Belt and Road partners. New direct routes to the US West Coast, the Black Sea and India suggest the markets port operators are targeting. Typical products include vehicles and parts, electronics, robots, optical fibre and pharmaceuticals.

Challenges Tianjin manufacturers face online

These are common problems on manufacturers’ export websites, tied to the industry mix above. They are general observations, not statistics about Tianjin.

  • Tier-2 invisibility. Component and material suppliers to large OEMs often have almost no searchable product information.
  • Regulated products. Pharma and aerospace suppliers must publish precise, approved wording.
  • Multinational comparison. Buyers compare Tianjin suppliers with global suppliers in the same zone.
  • Separate technical and sales paths. Engineers’ questions and price requests often go to the same inbox and get lost.

Questions overseas buyers ask Tianjin suppliers

These are typical questions in a first exchange with an overseas buyer, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Where do you sit in the supply chain?

Say whether you supply materials, components, subsystems or finished products, and to which tiers.

Which quality approvals do you hold?

List them with scope and certifying body.

How do you handle special processes and traceability?

Describe heat treatment, coating or clean-room processes and lot traceability.

Can you export through Tianjin Port on our schedule?

Explain lead times, packaging and the trade terms you quote.

Who handles regulatory documents?

Name the team responsible for export and regulatory documents.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for Tianjin manufacturers

  1. Write a short “where we fit” page explaining your role in your industrial chain, linked to the products for each role.
  2. Give each product or material grade a page with specifications, standards and the documents available.
  3. Publish quality and compliance information with scope and dates, and keep it current.
  4. Write for the reviewers: engineers, quality and regulatory staff each need a page that answers their questions.
  5. Register the site in Search Console. Google’s guide to getting started with Search Console explains how to see which searches show your pages.
  6. Set up separate inquiry paths for technical questions and quotes, so they reach the right person.

Does Hurfi have an office in Tianjin?

No. Hurfi has no office or staff in Tianjin. We work with Tianjin manufacturers remotely by WeChat, email and video, from Xi’an and Bogura.

We are a tier-2 supplier. Do overseas buyers search for companies like us?

Yes, especially engineers looking for specific components or materials. Clear specification pages make you findable for those searches.

How do we present aerospace or pharma approvals?

Exactly as issued: the standard, scope, certifying body and dates. Never imply approvals you do not hold.

Should we publish material data sheets?

Publish the properties that help buyers shortlist you, with test methods. Keep customer-specific data for after an NDA.
