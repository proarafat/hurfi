What Beijing makes, according to official data

Beijing’s 2025 statistical bulletin gives the figures below.

  • Beijing’s GDP was 5,207.34 billion yuan in 2025, up 5.4%; industry made up a small share, with the tertiary sector at 86.0% of GDP.[1]
  • Value added of industry above designated size grew 6.5%: computers, communications and electronic equipment grew 20.2% and automobile manufacturing 17.7%, while pharmaceuticals fell 8.6%.[1]
  • Industrial sales output was 2,778.70 billion yuan (+6.2%) and export delivery value 211.34 billion yuan (+6.4%).[1]
  • Output of new-energy vehicles rose 137.5%, lithium-ion batteries 117.6%, service robots 47.6% and wind turbines 21.6%.[1]
  • Beijing’s goods exports were 628.68 billion yuan, up 3.8%, while total trade fell 11.5% to 3,192.97 billion yuan.[1]

Beijing’s manufacturers are often research-driven and sell products that need explaining: robots, batteries, instruments, medical devices and electronics. Their buyers are engineers, integrators and institutions. They want technical depth, evidence and a credible company story — and they judge all three from the website.

Challenges Beijing manufacturers face online

These are common problems on manufacturers’ export websites, tied to the industry mix above. They are general observations, not statistics about Beijing.

  • Too much jargon. Research-led companies often write for peers; buyers need plain benefits and clear specifications.
  • Big claims without evidence. “World-leading” means little without data, test results or references.
  • AI search ignored. Pages without clear facts and structure are less likely to be understood and cited.
  • Contact paths for investors, not buyers. Many tech sites route every inquiry to investor relations.

Questions overseas buyers ask Beijing suppliers

These are typical questions in a first exchange with an overseas buyer, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Is this technology proven in our application?

Describe tested applications and published results, and what is still in development.

Which certifications and registrations do you hold?

List them per market with scope and validity.

How does integration work?

Publish interfaces, protocols, SDKs and documentation.

Who supports us after purchase?

Explain support channels, response times you can actually meet, and partners abroad.

Can we run a pilot?

Say whether pilots or evaluation units are possible and how they work.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for Beijing manufacturers

  1. Write application pages that explain the problem, how the product solves it and the evidence — test data, standards, published papers.
  2. Publish technical documentation as web pages: interfaces, protocols and specifications that engineers can read before they contact you.
  3. Add a page for researchers and institutions if you sell to labs, universities or hospitals, with procurement documents they usually need.
  4. Show your team and R&D honestly: real people, real labs, real patents with numbers that can be checked.
  5. Read Google’s guidance on AI features: clear, well-structured pages are what AI search and normal search both reward.
  6. Track demo requests, pilot inquiries and documentation downloads separately.

Does Hurfi have an office in Beijing?

No. Hurfi does not have an office or staff in Beijing. We work with Beijing companies remotely by WeChat, email and video, from our offices in Xi’an and Bogura.

We are a research-led company. Is marketing language a problem?

Often, yes. Engineers distrust hype. We write plainly and support claims with data and standards.

Should we publish technical documentation openly?

Publish what helps buyers evaluate you; keep sensitive details behind a request. A balance usually brings more qualified inquiries.

How do we market medical or lab equipment abroad?

Carefully: state registrations and intended use exactly, and let your regulatory team approve every page.

Not fundamentally. Clear, accurate, well-structured pages work for both normal and AI search.
