What Shanghai makes, according to official data

The Shanghai Municipal Bureau of Statistics publishes an annual bulletin and an earlier review of the year. The 2025 figures below come from both.

  • Output of industrial enterprises above designated size was 4,068.7 billion yuan in 2025, up 4.6%.[1]
  • Output of strategic emerging industries was 1,829.7 billion yuan, 45.0% of above-designated-size industrial output.[1]
  • By sector, output of railway, ship, aerospace and other transport equipment rose 15.8%, electrical machinery 11.1%, automobiles 7.8% and electronic equipment 7.7%.[2]
  • Integrated-circuit manufacturing grew 15.1%, AI-related manufacturing 13.6%, new energy 12.9% and high-end equipment 11.1%.[2]
  • Goods exports were 2.02 trillion yuan, up 10.8%; exports of the “new three” products were 0.16 trillion yuan, up 17.4%, with electric-vehicle exports up 13.8%.[2]

Shanghai’s growth is concentrated in technically demanding sectors where buyers run supplier qualification before they buy. Engineers, quality teams and compliance staff each read your website with different questions. A good Shanghai supplier site gives each of them a page to find their answer.

Shanghai’s main industries and what buyers compare

The clusters below follow the official figures above. Each links to our page for that industry.

Semiconductors and electronics

IC and electronics suppliers are evaluated on process, reliability data and supply continuity. Publish what you can and say what is available under NDA.

Aerospace and marine equipment

These buyers look for quality-system approvals, traceability and the programmes you are allowed to mention. Precision matters more than volume of text.

Automotive and new energy

Vehicle, battery and solar suppliers need test standards, certification by market and production capacity stated plainly.

High-end equipment

Equipment buyers expect specifications, installation references and a clear service model in their region.

Industrial zones and districts in Shanghai

  • The Lin-gang Special Area of the China (Shanghai) Pilot Free Trade Zone was set up under a State Council plan issued on 6 August 2019 and covers 819 square kilometres.[4]
  • Its industrial policies focus on four priority industries — integrated circuits, artificial intelligence, biomedicine and aerospace — and also support high-end equipment, intelligent connected vehicles, new materials and new energy.[4]

Lin-gang is one of several Shanghai zones with a technology focus. Suppliers to chip, aerospace and biomedical manufacturers there face strict qualification, which is why their websites need technical, quality and compliance detail.

Ports, logistics and trade fairs

  • Shanghai’s port handled 885.38 million tonnes of cargo (+2.5%) and 55.06 million TEU (+6.9%) in 2025; 53.0% of containers were water-to-water transshipment.[1]
  • Pudong and Hongqiao airports handled 4.54 million tonnes of cargo and mail (+7.9%), 4.10 million tonnes of it at Pudong.[1]

Buyers comparing suppliers ask how goods will reach them, how long it takes and on what trade terms. Shipping ports, typical lead times and Incoterms on your website answer that before it is asked.

Export profile, typical products and buyer markets

  • Total goods trade was 4,510.3 billion yuan (+5.6%), with imports up 1.8%.[1]
  • By market, exports to the EU were 325.7 billion yuan (+8.5%) and to ASEAN 322.1 billion yuan (+26.5%).[1]

Exports to ASEAN grew more than three times as fast as exports to the EU in 2025, and the two are now similar in size. Typical exports follow the bulletin: electric vehicles and batteries, electrical machinery, integrated circuits, and transport equipment. Websites aimed at both regions should be explicit about standards, certifications and support arrangements per market.

Challenges Shanghai manufacturers face online

These are common problems on manufacturers’ export websites, tied to the industry mix above. They are general observations, not statistics about Shanghai.

  • Qualification, not discovery, is the bottleneck. Buyers already know Shanghai; what slows them down is missing quality, compliance and capacity information.
  • Multiple evaluators. Engineers, quality staff and purchasing each look for different evidence, and most sites only serve one of them.
  • IP sensitivity. Suppliers must show capability without exposing customer designs.
  • International site structure. Companies serving several markets often have duplicated, inconsistent country sites.

Questions overseas buyers ask Shanghai suppliers

These are typical questions in a first exchange with an overseas buyer, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Will you pass our supplier qualification?

Publish your certifications with scope, your quality manual summary and the audit types you have passed, where you are allowed to say so.

Can you provide reliability or test data?

Show typical test results and the standards used; offer full reports under NDA.

How do you protect our IP?

Explain NDA practice, data access, and how drawings are stored and shared.

Is your capacity enough for our programme?

State capacity ranges and how you scale, rather than claiming unlimited capacity.

Who are your engineering counterparts?

Introduce the people who handle technical questions, with roles and languages.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for Shanghai manufacturers

  1. Create pages for each evaluator: a technical page for engineers, a quality page for quality teams, and a compliance page for certifications and export documents.
  2. Use exact names for standards and approvals, and the issuing body. Do not use logos of approvals you do not hold.
  3. Write application pages that explain where your product is used and what problem it solves, with figures you can stand behind.
  4. If you serve buyers in several countries, choose a clear URL structure. Google’s guide to multi-regional and multilingual sites compares country domains, subdomains and subdirectories.
  5. Put a named contact and a short response promise on every product page, so a qualified buyer knows who will answer.
  6. Measure RFQs and document requests separately; a datasheet request from an engineer is a different signal from a price request.

Questions from Shanghai manufacturers

Does Hurfi have an office in Shanghai?

No. Hurfi has no office or staff in Shanghai. We work with Shanghai companies remotely by WeChat, email and video, from our offices in Xi’an and Bogura.

We already sell to global companies. What does a better website add?

It shortens supplier qualification for new buyers and makes you findable for new projects. Engineers often check a supplier’s site before agreeing to a first call.

How much technical detail should we publish?

Enough for a buyer to decide you are worth a call: key specifications, standards and capabilities. Keep confidential data for after an NDA, and say on the page that it is available.

Should we use a .com, a country domain or subfolders?

For most exporters a single .com with language or country subfolders is simplest to run. Google documents the trade-offs of each option.

Can you write for aerospace or semiconductor buyers?

Yes, from your own documents and with your engineers’ review. We do not invent specifications, approvals or customer names.

Can you mention our customers on the site?

Only with written permission. Otherwise we describe the application and sector without naming the customer.

Do you run paid campaigns too?

Yes, where they make sense — usually for specific product searches in specific countries, measured by RFQs from your own accounts.

Can you promise results by a set date?

No. Search results depend on competition and your current site. We set milestones, report progress monthly and change the plan when the data says so.
