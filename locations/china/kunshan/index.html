Export websites and Google SEO for Kunshan manufacturers

What Kunshan makes, according to official data

The figures below come from Kunshan’s 2025 statistical bulletin, published in May 2026.

  • Kunshan’s GDP was 561.534 billion yuan in 2025, up 5.8%: second-industry value added was 289.057 billion yuan (+6.4%) and services 269.835 billion yuan (+5.1%), a 0.5:51.4:48.1 split.[1]
  • Industrial output above designated size reached 1,302.79 billion yuan (+4.9%); high-tech output was 622.077 billion yuan, 47.7% of the total.[1]
  • The city has one IT cluster worth more than 100 billion yuan — computers, communications and other electronic equipment — and eleven clusters worth more than 10 billion yuan each. Sixteen firms passed 10 billion yuan of output and 1,147 passed 100 million.[1]
  • Output in 2025 included 16.372 million computers, 72.169 million mobile phones and 102 million LCD panels.[1]
  • Trade was 824.80 billion yuan (+1.3%), with exports of 536.94 billion yuan, flat on 2024. General trade rose 8.6% to 174.793 billion yuan; processing trade fell 0.7% to 495.303 billion yuan.[1]
  • Cross-border e-commerce trade was 1.992 billion yuan, up 335.2%, and 80 new foreign-invested projects were signed, with 1.03 billion US dollars of registered foreign capital and 792 million US dollars actually used.[1]

Kunshan’s trade is still dominated by processing: about three-fifths of it is contract manufacturing for brands based elsewhere. Those brands, and their distributors, choose suppliers by reading — specifications, certifications, capacity and quality systems — before they ever send a drawing. The city also has more than 3,500 valid high-tech enterprises and 28,238 valid invention patents, so many suppliers can answer technical questions in detail. The website is where that detail has to be visible. Cross-border e-commerce grew almost fourfold in 2025, which means more Kunshan makers are now selling direct and need product pages that work for buyers who never visit China.

The clusters below follow the official figures above. Each links to our page for that industry.

Electronics, displays and IT hardware

Precision parts, moulds and tooling

Equipment and automation

Brands look for process capability, IP handling, audit readiness and realistic lead times.

Ports, logistics and trade fairs

  • The Yangtze River Delta International Airport Kunshan Logistics Centre, a bonded air-cargo station linked to Shanghai’s airports, handled 16.175 billion yuan of imports and exports.[1]
  • Kunshan Comprehensive Bonded Zone had 44 firms in the VAT general-taxpayer pilot, with 587.31 billion yuan invoiced under the scheme.[1]
  • Courier volume passed 1.4 billion parcels (+23.4%), the highest of any county-level city in Jiangsu.[1]

Buyers comparing suppliers ask how goods will reach them, how long it takes and on what trade terms. Shipping ports, typical lead times and Incoterms on your website answer that before it is asked.

Export profile, typical products and buyer markets

  • Taiwanese-invested firms remain central: 203 of them used the cross-group renminbi lending pilot, borrowing and lending 32.607 billion yuan and saving more than 1 billion yuan in financing costs.[1]
  • Eighty new foreign-invested projects were signed in 2025, with 1.03 billion US dollars of registered foreign capital and 792 million US dollars actually used.[1]
  • The city counted more than 3,500 valid high-tech enterprises, 23 Jiangsu unicorns or potential unicorns and 124 gazelle companies.[1]

Kunshan sells to brand owners in the US, Europe and Japan through processing trade, and increasingly direct to buyers in Southeast Asia and Belt and Road markets through cross-border e-commerce. Both groups research suppliers online first, but they look for different things: brands want audit and process evidence, direct buyers want product data, prices and shipping.

Challenges Kunshan manufacturers face online

These are common problems on manufacturers’ export websites, tied to the industry mix above. They are general observations, not statistics about Kunshan.

  • One page for everything. Buyers cannot tell product families apart.
  • Specifications locked in PDFs. Search engines and buyers both miss them.
  • Certifications implied, not listed. Say which markets, which scope.
  • No logistics answer. Airport, port and bonded options are a buying criterion.

Questions overseas buyers ask Kunshan suppliers

These are typical questions in a first exchange with an overseas buyer, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Can you show the exact panel, module or chipset we would receive?

Name the part numbers you build around and put the specification table in page text, not only in a PDF.

Which certifications cover my market?

List them by market with scope, certificate number and the products covered.

Can you manufacture under our brand and packaging?

Explain your OEM and ODM process, tooling ownership and how you protect drawings.

What is your monthly capacity and lead time?

Give realistic ranges per product family and say what changes them.

How do you ship from Kunshan?

Name the airport, port and bonded options you actually use, with typical terms.

Can your engineers work from our specification?

Describe sampling, DFM feedback and how new projects are quoted.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for Kunshan manufacturers

  1. Put what you make, for which industry and to which standards on the first screen.
  2. Give every product family a page with an HTML specification table and part numbers.
  3. Publish a certifications page by market and link it from each product page.
  4. Write a logistics page: airport and port options, packing, Incoterms and lead times.
  5. Show production, test and inspection with real photos and short captions.
  6. Track RFQs by product family and country, and review them monthly.

Does Hurfi have an office in Kunshan?

No. Hurfi does not have an office or staff in Kunshan. We work with Kunshan manufacturers remotely by WeChat, email and video, from our offices in Xi’an and Bogura.

We mostly do processing trade for foreign brands. Do we need a website?

Yes, but a different one. Your buyers are brand owners and their sourcing teams, so the site should show process capability, quality systems, audit readiness and capacity — not retail-style product listings.

We make displays and IT hardware. What should the site show?

Panel and module specifications, interfaces, certifications, test data and production capacity, with part numbers in the page text so buyers and search engines can match them.

Can you write for OEM and ODM work without exposing our customers?

Yes. We describe capability and process, never client names or drawings unless you approve them in writing.
