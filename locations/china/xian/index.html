What Xi’an makes, according to official data

The figures below come from the Xi’an government’s summary of the city’s 2025 economic performance, based on statistics bureau data.

  • Value added of industrial enterprises above designated size grew 5.7% in 2025. Automobile manufacturing grew 20.1%, electrical machinery and equipment 28.5%, special-purpose equipment 7.6% and railway, ship, aerospace and other transport equipment 8.4%.[1]
  • Total output of nine industrial chain groups, including passenger cars and intelligent connected vehicles, photovoltaics and commercial vehicles, grew 14.0%.[1]
  • Output of mobile-communication base-station equipment rose 279.1%, civil drones 78.5%, 3D printing equipment 71.3%, charging piles 43.6%, urban rail vehicles 44.6%, lithium-ion batteries 13.4% and solar cells 11.4%.[1]
  • Exports were 349.26 billion yuan, up 25.8%; mechanical and electrical exports rose 25.6% and electric passenger-car exports 66.1%.[1]
  • China–Europe Railway Express (Xi’an) ran 6,037 trains, up 21.1%.[1]

Xi’an’s exporters are newer to overseas markets than many coastal factories, and exports grew fast in 2025. Many still rely on a Chinese website or a marketplace listing. An English website built for overseas buyers, and search visibility for specific products, are often the first steps.

Xi’an’s main industries and what buyers compare

The clusters below follow the official figures above. Each links to our page for that industry.

Automotive and EVs

Vehicle and parts makers should show specifications, test standards, certification per market and export experience.

Solar and new energy

Solar and battery suppliers are compared on efficiency, warranty, certificates and documents.

Aerospace and drones

Aerospace and drone suppliers need careful pages on approvals, export control and specifications.

Electrical equipment and electronics

Electrical and communications-equipment suppliers should publish ratings, standards and test reports.

Industrial zones and districts in Xi’an

  • The Xi’an High-tech Industries Development Zone, established in March 1991 in the first batch of national high-tech zones, reports more than 191,000 registered enterprises and core industries including electronic information (semiconductors and software) and advanced manufacturing for new-energy vehicles.[4]
  • The Xi’an Yanliang National Aviation Hi-Tech Industrial Base was upgraded in 2010 to the National Shaanxi Aviation Economic and Technological Development Zone, which the city describes as the only national development zone featuring aviation.[4]
  • The Xi’an International Trade and Logistic Park covers 120 square kilometres and includes the Xi’an Railway Container Freight Station and the Xi’an Comprehensive Free Trade Zone.[4]

Semiconductors and EVs in the high-tech zone, aviation in Yanliang, aerospace in the civil aerospace base, and rail freight at the international port area: each zone has its own buyers and its own compliance questions.

Ports, logistics and trade fairs

  • China–Europe Railway Express (Xi’an) ran 6,037 trains in 2025 (+21.1%), carrying 6.96 million tonnes of goods (+23.7%).[1]

Buyers comparing suppliers ask how goods will reach them, how long it takes and on what trade terms. Shipping ports, typical lead times and Incoterms on your website answer that before it is asked.

Export profile, typical products and buyer markets

  • Trade with Belt and Road partner countries was 267.78 billion yuan (+11.6%); trade with ASEAN grew 21.8% and with the EU 47.4%.[1]
  • High-tech product exports grew 26.3%.[1]

Xi’an’s trade with the EU grew by nearly half in 2025, helped by rail links to Europe, and ASEAN trade also grew fast. Typical exports include electric passenger cars and parts, solar cells, lithium batteries, base-station and electrical equipment, and aviation products. EU buyers will look for conformity and certification information on the product pages.

Challenges Xi’an manufacturers face online

These are common problems on manufacturers’ export websites, tied to the industry mix above. They are general observations, not statistics about Xi’an.

  • New to export. Many Xi’an manufacturers have Chinese-only sites or rely on marketplace listings.
  • Translation instead of localisation. Translated Chinese sites rarely answer overseas buyers’ questions.
  • Controlled technologies. Aerospace and drone suppliers must be careful about what technical data they publish.
  • Rail logistics unexplained. Buyers in Europe may not know rail freight options unless the site explains them.

Questions overseas buyers ask Xi’an suppliers

These are typical questions in a first exchange with an overseas buyer, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Have you exported to our region before?

State the regions you have shipped to and the documents you prepared, without naming clients unless allowed.

How do you ship to Europe — by sea or rail?

Explain the options you use and typical transit times you can stand behind.

Are your products certified for our market?

List certificates per model and market.

Who will speak to us in English?

Name the English-speaking contact and their role.

Can we visit the factory?

Explain how visits work and whether video tours are possible.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for Xi’an manufacturers

  1. Build an English site written for overseas buyers, not a translation of the Chinese site. Start with your main products and the questions buyers ask about them.
  2. Give each product line a page with specifications in text, certificates and photos you own.
  3. Add an export page covering shipping options, documents, payment terms and contact people.
  4. Use helpful, people-first content as the standard: written by people who know the product, for buyers, not to fill keywords.
  5. Set up Search Console and analytics from day one, so you can see which searches and pages bring buyers.
  6. Keep your LinkedIn company page consistent with the website, so a buyer checking both sees the same company.
