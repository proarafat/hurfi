B2B websites and SEO for Hangzhou manufacturers and tech suppliers

Hangzhou combines a large digital economy with fast-growing equipment, vehicle and robotics manufacturing. Its suppliers often sell hardware and software together, which is hard to explain on a catalogue site. Hurfi helps them explain it clearly in English and get found by overseas buyers.

What Hangzhou makes, according to official data

The figures below are from Hangzhou’s 2025 statistical bulletin, issued by the municipal statistics bureau and the national survey team in Hangzhou. We quote the text as published by the city’s official newspaper, because the bureau’s own listing loads only with scripts.

  • Value added of industrial enterprises above designated size grew 6.0% in 2025. Equipment manufacturing grew 9.4% and made up 57.2% of that value added.[1]
  • Automobile manufacturing value added grew 36.7% and computer, communications and other electronic equipment 13.4%.[1]
  • Output of new-energy vehicles rose 383.0%, industrial robots 38.6%, 3D printing equipment 15.1% and integrated circuits 11.7%.[1]
  • Value added of the core digital-economy industries was 678.0 billion yuan, 29.5% of the city’s GDP.[1]
  • Goods exports were 646.9 billion yuan, up 8.7%; mechanical and electrical exports were 310.7 billion yuan, up 10.9%, and exports to the EU 124.9 billion yuan, up 11.9%.[1]
  • Private enterprises made up 89.2% of above-designated-size industrial firms and 59.5% of their value added.[1]

Many Hangzhou exporters are private firms selling machines, devices or vehicles with software, data or connectivity built in. Overseas buyers need to understand both parts: what the hardware does and what the software adds. A website that splits those into separate, clear pages is easier to evaluate — and easier for search engines to rank for the right searches.

Hangzhou’s main industries and what buyers compare

The clusters below follow the official figures above. Each links to our page for that industry.

Equipment and automation

Equipment makers should show the machine, the control system and the integration options, with real installation photos and service terms by region.

EVs and vehicle parts

With vehicle output growing fast, parts and subsystem suppliers need pages per component, with test standards and the vehicle types they support.

Electronics and chips

Electronics suppliers are found by part type and parameter; HTML datasheets help engineers and search engines alike.

Industrial software and connected devices

Software-heavy suppliers need plain explanations of deployment, data location, integration and support — the questions IT and operations teams ask first.

Industrial zones and districts in Hangzhou

  • Binjiang District, which is also the Hangzhou High-tech Zone, covers 73 square kilometres; Alibaba, Geely and Hikvision are headquartered there, according to the city’s official English portal.[4]

Binjiang is the best-known of Hangzhou’s technology districts. Many hardware suppliers sell into the ecosystems around these companies, so their websites need to explain software, connectivity and integration as well as the hardware.

Ports, logistics and trade fairs

  • Xiaoshan International Airport connected 218 cities by the end of 2025, 54 of them international (six more than a year earlier), and handled 50.46 million passengers (+5.0%).[1]

Buyers comparing suppliers ask how goods will reach them, how long it takes and on what trade terms. Shipping ports, typical lead times and Incoterms on your website answer that before it is asked.

Export profile, typical products and buyer markets

  • By market, exports to Belt and Road partner countries were 320.7 billion yuan (+14.1%), about half of all goods exports.[1]
  • Private enterprises exported 498.0 billion yuan (+11.3%), 77.0% of the city’s goods exports; cross-border e-commerce exports were 152.3 billion yuan (+14.9%).[1]

Exports are growing fastest to Belt and Road partner markets, with the EU a large and growing destination. Typical export products follow the bulletin’s growth lines: new-energy vehicles, industrial robots, 3D printing equipment, integrated circuits and connected devices. EU buyers will look for conformity information; buyers in emerging markets often need clearer explanations of service and support.

Challenges Hangzhou manufacturers face online

These are common problems on manufacturers’ export websites, tied to the industry mix above. They are general observations, not statistics about Hangzhou.

  • Hardware plus software is hard to explain. Buyers struggle when a single page tries to sell a device, a platform and a service at once.
  • Data and security questions. Overseas IT and procurement teams ask where data is stored and how updates work before they talk to sales.
  • Fast product cycles. Pages go out of date quickly, leaving discontinued models and broken links.
  • Chinese-first content. Product pages written for domestic platforms often miss the terms overseas engineers search for.

Questions overseas buyers ask Hangzhou suppliers

These are typical questions in a first exchange with an overseas buyer, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Does the software come with the hardware, or separately?

Explain licensing, updates and what happens if the subscription ends, on a page of its own.

Where is our data stored, and who can access it?

State hosting options and access rules plainly. Vague answers stop procurement reviews.

Can it integrate with our existing systems?

List supported protocols, interfaces and example integrations you can actually show.

Who supports us in our time zone and language?

Describe support hours, languages and escalation, and whether you use local partners.

Which certifications cover the export version?

List the certificates per market and model, with the holder and scope.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for Hangzhou manufacturers

  1. Separate hardware, software and services into their own pages, each with a clear title, so a buyer searching for one does not land on a page about another.
  2. Write an integration page per system or protocol you support, with what works today. Avoid listing integrations that are only planned.
  3. Publish installation case descriptions with photos you own and client names only where you have written permission.
  4. Give support and data questions their own page — hosting, updates, response times — because procurement teams look for them before they talk to sales.
  5. If you sell to Europe, check you have the right documents for the market: for products that need CE marking, the European Commission states that manufacturers carry out the conformity assessment and issue the EU declaration of conformity. Say on the page which models carry it.
  6. Track demo requests and RFQs as separate conversions so you can see which pages produce which kind of inquiry.

Questions

Questions from Hangzhou manufacturers

Does Hurfi have an office in Hangzhou?

No. We do not have an office or staff in Hangzhou. We work remotely with Hangzhou companies by WeChat, email and video, from our offices in Xi’an and Bogura.

We sell hardware with software. How should the website handle that?

Give each its own clear pages — what the device does, what the software adds, how they connect — and link between them. Buyers search for each part separately.

Do overseas buyers care about our digital features?

Often yes, but they need specifics: what data is collected, where it is stored, how updates work and what happens offline. Unclear answers slow down procurement.

Our product changes quickly. How do we keep the site current?

Use a product structure that lets you update specifications in one place, mark discontinued models clearly and keep old URLs redirecting to the replacement.

Is a Chinese website enough if we use a translation plugin?

No. Machine-translated pages often get terms wrong and miss the searches buyers make. Write the English pages properly and, if you keep a Chinese version, connect them with hreflang.

Can you guarantee rankings for “robot” or “EV” keywords?

No one can guarantee rankings. Broad terms are also rarely the best target. We focus on specific product and application searches where a qualified buyer is likely.

Do you handle LinkedIn and trade content?

Yes. We plan and publish LinkedIn posts about real installations, engineering and team, in English, and link them to the relevant website pages.

How do you measure results?

Search Console and analytics in your own accounts: impressions, clicks, demo requests and RFQs by page. We do not report vanity reach as success.

What should we prepare before the first call?

Your current website, product list, the markets you want and any existing English material. If you do not have these, we start from what you have.
