What Nanjing makes, according to official data

The Nanjing Statistics Bureau’s review of the 2025 economy and the city’s 2025 statistical bulletin give the figures below.

  • Nanjing’s GDP was 1,942.88 billion yuan in 2025, up 5.2%, and value added of industry above designated size grew 5.8%, with 28 of 37 industry groups growing.[1]
  • Among key industries, general-purpose equipment grew 8.9%, pharmaceuticals 10.8% and instruments and meters 12.1%.[1]
  • High-tech industry output passed 900 billion yuan and made up 55.2% of above-designated-size industrial output; aerospace manufacturing output grew 17.7%.[1]
  • Output of new-energy vehicles rose 99.0%, integrated circuits 28.2%, industrial robots 35.4% and optoelectronic devices 66.6%.[1][2]
  • The bulletin lists 294,898 new-energy vehicles, 54,134 industrial robot sets and 27.09 billion integrated circuits produced in 2025.[2]

Nanjing’s growth is in the most specification-heavy parts of manufacturing: instruments, medicines, aerospace parts, robots and chips. Buyers in these fields do not shortlist a supplier from a slogan. They look for ranges, tolerances, standards, test methods and certificates — and they expect to find them on the product page, in text they can quote.

Nanjing’s main industries and what buyers compare

The clusters below follow the official figures above. Each links to our page for that industry.

Instruments and test equipment

Instrument makers should publish measuring ranges, accuracy classes, interfaces and calibration documents per model, with downloadable manuals.

Pharmaceuticals and bioprocess

Pharma buyers check registrations, GMP status and documentation per market before price. State them exactly as your regulatory team approves.

General equipment and machinery

Equipment buyers compare capacity, power, footprint, controls and service abroad. One page per machine family keeps comparison simple.

Robots and automation

Robot and automation suppliers win with payload, reach, repeatability, integration examples and support arrangements written out in full.

Electronics and semiconductors

Chip and optoelectronic suppliers should list packages, parameters, qualification standards and lead-time ranges in searchable text.

Buyers comparing suppliers ask how goods will reach them, how long it takes and on what trade terms. Shipping ports, typical lead times and Incoterms on your website answer that before it is asked.

Challenges Nanjing manufacturers face online

These are common problems on manufacturers’ export websites, tied to the industry mix above. They are general observations, not statistics about Nanjing.

  • Specifications locked in PDFs and images. Engineers cannot search or copy them, and Google cannot read them well.
  • One site for every audience. Distributors, OEM customers and end users need different pages; a single generic catalogue serves none of them well.
  • Compliance claims without detail. “Certified” with no standard, scope or market named raises more questions than it answers.
  • No measurement. Without Search Console and conversion tracking, nobody knows which products attract serious interest.

Questions overseas buyers ask Nanjing suppliers

These are typical questions in a first exchange with an overseas buyer, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Which standards does this instrument or machine meet?

Name each standard and certificate on the product page, with the market it applies to.

Can we see the datasheet before we talk?

Publish datasheets as HTML pages with a PDF download, not only as images.

Who will support us after installation?

Explain remote support, spare parts and any service partners abroad, honestly.

Do you customise for OEM projects?

Describe what can change — interfaces, housings, firmware, labelling — and what stays fixed.

How long do samples and first orders take?

Give typical ranges for samples, first batches and repeat orders.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for Nanjing manufacturers

  1. Build a page per product family with the parameters engineers filter by — range, accuracy, power, interface — in HTML tables, not images.
  2. Publish a quality and compliance page that lists certificates, test equipment and inspection steps, and link it from every product page.
  3. Write application pages (for example “flow meters for water treatment”) that explain where each product fits and which specifications matter.
  4. Give regulated products — medicines, medical and lab equipment — wording that your regulatory team has approved, and keep a dated version history.
  5. Follow Google’s helpful-content guidance: write for the engineer who has to justify the purchase, not for search engines.
  6. Track datasheet downloads and RFQ submissions as separate conversions so you can see which products attract serious interest.
