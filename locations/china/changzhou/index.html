What Changzhou makes, according to official data

The figures below come from Changzhou’s 2025 statistical bulletin and a city government update on the new-energy industry.

  • Value added of industrial enterprises above designated size grew 4.6% in 2025. Electronics output rose 12.3% and biomedicine 5.1%; output of power and energy-storage batteries rose 14.1%.[1]
  • High-tech manufacturing output exceeded 300 billion yuan, up 12.3%.[1]
  • The city government reports a new-energy industry of 1,047.9 billion yuan in 2025, with the NEV and parts cluster above 500 billion yuan and more than 800,000 new-energy vehicles produced.[2]
  • The same update gives power-battery capacity of 212.9 GWh and says Changzhou makes 13% of China’s transformers.[2]
  • Goods exports were 304.71 billion yuan, up 17.0%.[1]

Battery, EV and power-equipment buyers abroad are cautious: they need safety tests, transport certificates, market approvals and evidence of capacity before they talk price. In a city with many new-energy suppliers, the one whose website answers those questions first is often the one that gets the call.

Changzhou’s main industries and what buyers compare

Transformers and power equipment

Industrial zones and districts in Changzhou

  • Changzhou’s official English investment page calls the city the “New Energy Capital” and the “World Capital of Transformers”, and says its energy-storage battery industry chain is 97% complete, ranking first in China.[4]
  • The same page lists focus industries by district: power and next-generation batteries in Liyang and Jintan; electric drive components, automotive electronics and chips in Wujin; and new-energy vehicles, photovoltaics and energy storage in Xinbei (Changzhou National High-Tech District).[4]

Changzhou’s districts each promote different parts of the new-energy chain. A supplier’s district often signals what it makes, but buyers abroad still need the website to spell it out.

Ports, logistics and trade fairs

  • Changzhou’s port handled 130.07 million tonnes of cargo in 2025 (+0.8%).[1]

Buyers comparing suppliers ask how goods will reach them, how long it takes and on what trade terms. Shipping ports, typical lead times and Incoterms on your website answer that before it is asked.

Export profile, typical products and buyer markets

  • Total goods trade was 372.81 billion yuan (+13.9%), with imports up 1.7%.[1]
  • General trade made up 85.6% of trade, and private enterprises’ trade grew 25.6% to a 63.7% share.[1]
  • Civil drone output doubled, and output of energy-storage lithium-ion batteries rose 77.4%, carbon fibre and composites 46.6%, industrial robots 25.6% and transformers 16.2%.[1]

Exports grew 17% in 2025, driven mainly by private, general-trade exporters — companies selling their own products rather than processing for others. Typical products are batteries and storage systems, EVs and parts, PV modules, transformers and power equipment. Buyers in Europe and elsewhere check safety and grid certifications before anything else.

Challenges Changzhou manufacturers face online

These are common problems on manufacturers’ export websites, tied to the industry mix above. They are general observations, not statistics about Changzhou.

  • Certification-first buyers. Battery, PV and transformer buyers need test and certification data before price, and it is often missing online.
  • Crowded supply base. Many new-energy suppliers in one city make it hard to stand out without specific data.
  • Fast-growing private exporters. Companies scaling quickly often lack English content and RFQ processes.
  • Multilingual markets. Selling into several countries means language versions that are often inconsistent or unconnected.

Questions overseas buyers ask Changzhou suppliers

These are typical questions in a first exchange with an overseas buyer, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Which safety and transport tests has this product passed?

List the test standards and reports per model, with dates.

Which certifications apply in our market?

Show market-specific certificates and who holds them.

What is your real production capacity?

Give honest capacity ranges and lead times.

What warranty and degradation terms do you offer?

State warranty terms clearly, with the conditions that apply.

Can we witness factory acceptance tests?

Explain whether and how buyers can witness tests, in person or by video.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for Changzhou manufacturers

  1. Create a page per product family with specifications, test standards and certification status per market.
  2. Publish a safety and compliance page that collects test reports and certificates, linked from each product.
  3. Add a capacity and quality page: lines, automation level, inspection and testing, with photos you own.
  4. Explain warranty and service in plain words, including how claims are handled abroad.
  5. Plan English and other language pages with Google’s advice on localized versions: each language version should list itself and the others, and links must work both ways or they are ignored.
  6. Track datasheet downloads and RFQs separately, so you can see technical interest and buying interest.

Questions from Changzhou manufacturers

Does Hurfi have an office in Changzhou?

No. Hurfi has no office or staff in Changzhou. We work with Changzhou manufacturers remotely by WeChat, email and video, from our offices in Xi’an and Bogura.

Battery buyers ask for many documents. Should we publish them?

Publish the documents that help buyers shortlist you — test standards, certificates, transport documents — and offer full reports on request.

We sell to several countries. Do we need several languages?

Only where you can answer buyers in that language. English first, then other languages with proper hreflang.

How do we show capacity without exaggerating?

Give ranges, explain the lines and shifts, and avoid claims like “unlimited capacity”. Buyers check.

Can you write about transformers and power equipment?

Yes, from your ratings, type-test reports and standards, reviewed by your engineers.

Do you handle solar product pages?

Yes: efficiency, warranty, certifications and documents per market, presented clearly.

Can you help with LinkedIn?

Yes. LinkedIn helps buyers check the company and the people who contact them. We plan posts about real production and testing.

No. We aim for qualified inquiries and report them from your own accounts.
