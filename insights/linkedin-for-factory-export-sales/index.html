LinkedIn for factory export sales: a practical guide

When a factory’s sales manager emails an overseas buyer, the buyer often looks up the person and the company on LinkedIn before replying. A blank or inconsistent profile costs trust. This guide covers what a manufacturer needs on LinkedIn and how to keep it working without fabricating anything.

Why LinkedIn matters to overseas buyers

LinkedIn is where many procurement and engineering staff check a supplier and the person contacting them. They look for consistency: does the company page match the website, does the salesperson work there, and is there evidence of real production?

A LinkedIn presence does not replace a website. It is a second place to check you, and it should point to the website for details.

Set up the company page properly

LinkedIn Help explains how to create a LinkedIn Page for a company. Use your English company name exactly as it appears on your website and documents, your website URL, your real location and a short description of what you make.

  • Logo and cover image you own
  • Description that names your products and the industries you serve
  • Website link to the English site
  • Location matching your factory address
  • At least two admins from your company

Make sales profiles consistent

Each person who contacts buyers should have a profile with their real name, a professional photo, their role and the company page linked as their employer. Buyers notice when a salesperson’s profile is empty or lists a different company.

Post what only your factory can show

The most useful posts show things buyers cannot see elsewhere: a new line being installed, a test being run, an engineer explaining a design choice, packaging for an export order (without client names). One genuine post a week beats daily generic content.

  • Production and testing photos or short videos you own
  • Trade-show dates and booth details for confirmed fairs
  • Certifications when you receive them, with scope
  • Introductions to engineers and sales staff
  • Links to new product pages on your website

Reach out without spamming

Connection requests and messages work best when they are specific: why you are contacting this person, what you make that is relevant to them, and a link to the right product page. Mass messages with no context damage your reputation and can restrict your account.

Measure what matters

Track replies, meetings and RFQs that started on LinkedIn, and visits from LinkedIn to your website in analytics. Follower counts are less important than whether the right buyers engage.

A weekly routine for a small export team

LinkedIn works best as a steady habit, not a campaign. For a factory with one or two export sales staff, an hour or two a week is enough if it is spent on the right things.

  • Monday: post one piece of factory content — a production step, a test, a shipment — with a short explanation in English.
  • Tuesday–Thursday: reply to comments and messages within a working day; accept relevant connection requests.
  • Weekly: send a few personalised messages to buyers or engineers who engaged with your posts or visited your profile.
  • Monthly: review which posts led to profile visits, website clicks or conversations, and do more of those.

What to post when you cannot show customers

Many factories cannot name customers or show their parts. That does not leave you with nothing to post. Buyers want to see process, quality and people, and you can show those without revealing a client.

  • Inspection equipment in use, with what it measures and how often.
  • Before-and-after examples of a process improvement, without customer names.
  • Certification audits passed, new equipment installed, capacity added.
  • Short explanations of a material, tolerance or standard your buyers ask about.
  • Your team: the engineer who answers technical questions, the person who handles shipping.

Connect LinkedIn to your website

Every LinkedIn post should have somewhere to go. Link to the matching product or capability page on your website, not just the home page. On the website, make the RFQ form short and clear, so a buyer who clicks through from LinkedIn can ask a question in a minute.

Use tagged links (UTM parameters) on the links you post, so your analytics show which LinkedIn posts lead to visits and inquiries. Without that, it is hard to tell whether LinkedIn is working.

Mistakes that make factories look unreliable

  • A company page with no logo, no website link or a description copied from a marketplace.
  • Sales profiles with different company names, job titles in Chinese only, or no photo.
  • Connection requests followed immediately by a price list.
  • Posting only product photos with “best price, contact us”, and nothing about process or quality.
  • Long gaps between posts, so the page looks abandoned when a buyer checks it.

Each of these is easy to fix, and fixing them matters because buyers check LinkedIn to confirm that a supplier is real before they reply.

Questions

Common questions

Do B2B buyers really use LinkedIn to check suppliers?

Many procurement and engineering staff do, especially before replying to someone they do not know. A consistent profile reduces doubt.

Should we create a company page or only personal profiles?

Both. The company page shows the organisation; personal profiles show who buyers will deal with. Link them.

What should we post?

Real production, testing, engineering and team content you own, plus new products with links to your website. Avoid stock images and generic slogans.

How often should we post?

Regularly enough to look active — often one genuine post a week is enough for a factory.

Can we buy followers or connections?

No. Fake followers do not buy anything and can damage trust if noticed.

Can we use LinkedIn in China?

Access depends on your network setup and LinkedIn’s services in your region. Many export teams manage their pages from outside China or through partners.

Should we name our customers in posts?

Only with written permission. Otherwise describe the application or industry.

How do we measure LinkedIn results?

Count replies, meetings and RFQs that came from LinkedIn, and LinkedIn visits to your site in analytics.

Can Hurfi manage our LinkedIn?

Yes. Our social media management service plans and publishes LinkedIn content with your team and links it to your website.
