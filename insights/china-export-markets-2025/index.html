Where Chinese manufacturing exports grew and fell in 2025 — and what it means for your website

Official 2025 statistical bulletins tell a clear story: exports to the United States fell in many places, while sales to the EU, ASEAN and Africa grew in several provinces and cities. This guide pulls together the figures we checked for our location guides and explains what they mean for an export website.

What the bulletins measure

Each Chinese province and city publishes an annual statistical bulletin (统计公报) with goods trade, exports and, often, exports by market. We use them because they are official and comparable. They describe whole regions, not your company: use them for context, then check your own orders and inquiries.

Inland provinces grew fastest

Several inland provinces reported strong export growth in 2025:

  • Hubei: exports up 19.6% to 581.86 billion yuan, with trade with the EU up 40.2%.
  • Henan: exports up 18.0% to 616.818 billion yuan.
  • Anhui: exports up 17.8% to 682.31 billion yuan; trade with ASEAN up 38.3% and with the EU 21.6%.

Much of this was driven by vehicles, batteries and electronics. Anhui’s bulletin, for example, reports vehicle exports of 1.228 million units and exports of electric vehicles, lithium-ion batteries and solar products up 107.8%.

Some coastal economies fell

Other bulletins show declines:

  • Fujian: exports down 6.7% to 1,155.362 billion yuan, with exports to the United States down 22.1% and to ASEAN 18.0% — but exports to the EU up 9.7%.
  • Fuzhou: exports down 32.0% to 174.73 billion yuan, falling in the US, EU and ASEAN.
  • Hunan: exports down 4.1%; exports to the United States down 29.2%, while exports to ASEAN rose 5.5%.
  • Sichuan: exports down 1.5% to 608.65 billion yuan.

The United States fell, other markets grew

City bulletins that list markets show the same pattern again and again:

  • Nantong: exports to the EU up 20.7%, ASEAN 20.9% and Africa 15.8%; to the United States down 19.3%.
  • Taizhou (Zhejiang): exports to the EU up 6.3%; to the United States down 14.4%.
  • Ningde: exports to the EU up 66.4%; to the United States down 13.5%.

For many exporters, the question in 2026 is not whether to look beyond the United States, but which markets to prepare for first.

Check your own data first

Regional figures do not tell you where your products sell. Before you plan anything, look at your own last two years:

  • Orders and revenue by country.
  • Website inquiries and RFQs by country.
  • Searches and clicks by country in Search Console.
  • Which countries ask for samples but do not order — and why.

Pick two or three markets you can really serve

A market is worth a page only if you can meet its rules and serve its buyers. For each candidate, ask:

  • Do we hold the certifications this market requires?
  • Can we quote the delivery terms buyers there expect?
  • Can we answer inquiries in time, in a language they use?
  • Do we have at least a few customers or references there?

Prepare the rules before the page

For the EU, for example, the European Commission explains that manufacturers must carry out the conformity assessment, prepare the technical file, issue the EU declaration of conformity and affix the CE marking where it applies (CE marking). A market page that promises compliance you do not have does more harm than no page at all.

Build market pages that help buyers

Good market pages are specific: the products you sell there, the standards and certificates that apply, the delivery terms you offer, references in the region and who will answer. Google’s guidance on managing multi-regional and multilingual sites explains the URL and language options if you add translated versions.

Measure, then adjust

Track inquiries by market from the first week. After three to six months you will know which markets respond and which need different products, prices or terms. Statistics point the way; your own data decides.

Questions

Common questions

Where do these figures come from?

From the 2025 statistical bulletins of each province or city, published by their statistics bureaus. Every figure is listed in the sources below.

Do these figures predict my sales?

No. They describe whole regions. Use them for context and check your own orders and inquiries.

Should we stop selling to the United States?

That is a business decision we cannot make for you. Many exporters are adding markets rather than leaving one.

Which markets should we target next?

The ones where you hold the right certifications, can quote the right terms and already have some customers or interest.

How many market pages should we build?

Start with two or three strong ones. Thin pages for every country rarely help buyers or search.

Do we need translated sites?

Only where buyers prefer another language and you can answer them in it.

Can Hurfi guarantee results in new markets?

No. Nobody can. We plan carefully and report honestly from your own data.
