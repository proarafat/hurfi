Market pages for buyers in ASEAN, the Middle East and Africa

Many Chinese manufacturers now sell more to ASEAN, the Middle East and Africa than before. Buyers in these regions search and compare suppliers online too, but they have their own questions. This guide explains how to build market pages that answer them — and how to avoid pages that only exist for search engines.

Why these regions matter now

Several 2025 bulletins show growth in these markets. Nantong’s exports to ASEAN rose 20.9% and to Africa 15.8%. Hunan’s exports to ASEAN rose 5.5% while its exports to the United States fell 29.2%. Weifang made 62% of its trade with Belt and Road countries, and Hubei 54.9%.

What buyers in these markets ask

Questions vary by country and product, but some come up again and again:

  • Can you deliver to my port or city, and on what terms?
  • Do you have distributors, service partners or spare parts nearby?
  • Which certifications or registrations does my country require, and do you hold them?
  • Can you supply in smaller quantities to start?
  • Can I talk to someone in my language or time zone?

What a good market page contains

  • The products you actually sell in that market, with links to their pages.
  • Standards, certificates and registrations that apply there — only those you hold.
  • Delivery terms, ports and typical transit times.
  • Service, spare parts and support arrangements.
  • References or projects in the region you are allowed to show.
  • Who answers inquiries, in which languages and hours.

What to avoid: doorway pages

Google’s spam policies describe doorway abuse: pages created to rank for similar searches that lead users to less useful intermediate pages, including pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page. A page for every country with the same text and a new name is exactly that. One strong page for a market you serve is worth more than fifty thin ones.

Languages: English first, then where it helps

English works for many B2B buyers in these regions, but Arabic, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Vietnamese, Thai or Indonesian pages can help where buyers prefer them. Translate only what you can support: if a buyer writes in Arabic, someone must be able to answer.

URLs and hreflang

Google’s guidance on multi-regional and multilingual sites compares country domains, subdomains and subdirectories. For most manufacturers, subdirectories such as /ar/ or /fr/ on one domain are simplest to manage.

When you have language or regional versions, Google recommends using hreflang to tell it about localized versions of a page, with an x-default for unmatched languages.

Proof that travels

Buyers far away need more proof, not less. Show real factory photos, testing, packing and loading; dated references in the region; and certificates with scope and validity. Avoid stock photos and claims you cannot support.

Connect the market page to the rest of the site

  • Link from the market page to each relevant product page, and back.
  • Link to your certifications and shipping pages.
  • Put a short, clear RFQ form on the page, with the questions buyers in that market usually answer.
  • Add the page to your XML sitemap.

An outline for one market page

  • Heading: your product type and the market, written for buyers, not keywords.
  • Two or three sentences on what you supply there and to whom.
  • Products sold in the market, linked.
  • Rules and certificates that apply, linked to your certifications page.
  • Delivery terms and ports, linked to your shipping page.
  • References and photos from the region.
  • Contact and RFQ form.

Measure by market

Track inquiries, sample requests and orders by country. Search Console shows queries and clicks by country. After a few months, keep improving the markets that respond and rethink the ones that do not.

Questions

Common questions

Should we build a page for every country?

No. Build strong pages for the markets you really serve. Thin, repeated country pages can count as doorway abuse under Google’s spam policies.

Is English enough for the Middle East and Africa?

Often for B2B, but Arabic or French pages can help in some markets — if you can answer inquiries in those languages.

What is hreflang?

An annotation that tells Google which pages are language or regional versions of each other, so it can show the right one.

Do we need local certifications?

Many markets have their own rules. Check with the authority in each market and list only certificates you hold.

Should we show prices for these markets?

Usually not exact prices, but MOQs, terms and lead times help buyers decide.

Can Hurfi translate our pages?

We plan and build language versions with proper translation and correct tags, and we review them with you.

Can you guarantee rankings in these markets?

No. Nobody can. We report honestly from your own Search Console.
