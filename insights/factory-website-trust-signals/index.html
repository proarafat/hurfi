Why overseas buyers hesitate on factory websites — and what to fix

Overseas buyers have learned to be careful. Many have met trading companies presented as factories, copied photos and certificates that did not check out. A real factory can lose an inquiry simply because its website looks like those. Here is what buyers look for, written from the factory’s side.

Start with who you are

The first signal is a clear, consistent identity:

  • Your registered company name in English, the same on every page, profile and document.
  • A real factory address, ideally with a map, and photos that match it.
  • Named people a buyer can contact, with roles — not only a generic form.
  • The year the company was founded and what you actually manufacture in-house.

If you are a trading company or work with partner factories, say so plainly. Buyers accept that much more easily than discovering it later.

Show the factory, not stock images

Stock photos of generic factories are recognised immediately. A few honest photos and short videos of your own production lines, inspection area and warehouse are worth more than a polished render. Name the equipment where it matters to the buyer — for example the machine types or test instruments you use.

Make certificates checkable

Certificates are only useful if a buyer can verify them. For each one, show:

  • The certificate itself, readable, not just a logo.
  • The certificate holder name, which should match your company name.
  • The scope — which products or sites it covers.
  • The issuing body and the validity date.

Do not show logos for certifications you do not hold, or imply that one model’s approval covers a whole range. Experienced buyers check, and a single mismatch can end the conversation.

Put the technical detail on the page

Specifications hidden in a downloadable catalog, or published as images, make a site feel thin. Put the key data in HTML tables on each product or capability page, with units and test conditions. This helps buyers compare you, and it helps search engines understand what you make.

Remove the warning signs

Some details make a real factory look unreliable. Check your site for:

  • Broken links, empty pages or “coming soon” sections.
  • Machine-translated text and inconsistent product names.
  • Claims with no evidence: “No.1 in China”, “world-leading”, unexplained star ratings.
  • Contact details that differ between the website, LinkedIn and marketplace profiles.
  • A free email address as the only contact, instead of an address on your own domain.

Explain what happens after an inquiry

A buyer is more likely to write if they know what comes next: who replies, within what time, whether you sign an NDA for drawings, how samples work and what information you need for a quotation. One short paragraph near the inquiry form removes much of the hesitation.

Keep it current

Out-of-date certificates, old product lines and copyright years from several years ago suggest a company that is not paying attention. Set a simple review cycle — for example every quarter — to check certificates, contact details and key product pages.

Questions

Common questions

Do we need to show client names?

No. Many factories cannot. Capability, process, equipment and quality evidence can build trust without naming clients.

Are reviews and ratings important?

Only if they are real and verifiable. An unexplained rating is a warning sign, not a trust signal.
