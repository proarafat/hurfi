How Chinese manufacturers can win more overseas buyer inquiries

Marketplaces and trade shows still matter. But many overseas buyers now start with a search, a LinkedIn profile or a recommendation — and then check the factory’s own website before they write. This guide explains how to be ready for that check.

Why a marketplace listing is not enough on its own

B2B marketplaces are useful: they bring traffic and a familiar way to request quotes. The problem is that every supplier on the platform looks similar. Your listing sits next to dozens of others, often with the same photos, the same claims and prices that invite a race to the bottom.

A buyer who is serious about a long-term supplier usually wants to know more than a listing can show: who you are, what you make yourself, what you outsource, which standards you work to and who they will deal with after the first order. That information belongs on a website you control.

What an overseas buyer checks before writing to you

Before an engineer or purchasing manager sends a first message, they are trying to answer a few simple questions:

  • Is this a real manufacturer, and where is the factory?
  • Do they make the product I need, to the specification and standard I need?
  • Can they handle my volumes, and what is the minimum order?
  • Who will answer me, in which language, and how quickly?
  • What happens after I send an inquiry?

If your website answers these questions in plain English, the buyer arrives at the first conversation already half-convinced. If it does not, many buyers simply move on without telling you why.

Build the website around those questions

A factory website that produces inquiries is usually less about design and more about structure:

  • Product or capability pages with specifications in HTML, not only in a PDF.
  • A factory and quality page with real photos, equipment, certificates and their scope.
  • Application or industry pages that speak to the buyer’s problem.
  • A clear inquiry path: what to send (drawings, quantities, target date) and what you will send back.

We go through the practical checklist in what a manufacturer website needs for RFQs.

Let search bring the right buyers

Google search works best for manufacturers when it targets the specific questions buyers ask at the specification stage — product types, materials, standards, tolerances, compatibility — rather than broad phrases such as “China manufacturer”, which mostly attract price shoppers.

This is slower than buying ads, and nobody can honestly promise a ranking. But pages that answer real technical questions keep working for years, and the buyers who arrive through them tend to be better qualified. Our SEO service is built around this approach.

Use LinkedIn to show the people and the process

Buyers want to know who they will deal with. A company page with no posts, or posts made only of stock images, does not help. Short posts that show real production steps, inspection, packing and the engineers behind the product are more convincing — as long as you only share what your customers allow you to share.

LinkedIn is also where many buyers will check the person who replies to their inquiry. Make sure that person’s profile is complete and consistent with the website. See social media management for how we approach this.

Answer quickly and consistently

All of this is wasted if an inquiry waits three days for a reply, or if the reply ignores the buyer’s question. Decide who answers inquiries, in which language, and within what time. Use the same company name, address and contact details on the website, LinkedIn and every marketplace profile, so a buyer who checks finds one consistent picture.

Measure what matters

Track qualified inquiries, not only traffic. Connect Google Search Console and a privacy-respecting analytics tool, note where each inquiry came from, and review it monthly. That tells you which pages and channels are worth more effort — and which are not.

Questions

Common questions

Should we stop using B2B marketplaces?

Not necessarily. Many factories use both. Your own website gives buyers a place to verify you and gives you inquiries that do not depend on one platform.

How long does it take for search to bring inquiries?

It depends on your market, competition and the state of your current site. Nobody can honestly promise a timeline; regular reporting shows whether it is working.
