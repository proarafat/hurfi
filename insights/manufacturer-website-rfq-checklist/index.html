What a manufacturer website needs before buyers send an RFQ

A request for quotation is a buyer telling you they are ready to compare prices. Your website can make that easy — or it can make the buyer give up halfway. This checklist covers the pages and details that help an overseas buyer send a complete RFQ.

1. Product or capability pages a buyer can compare

Every main product family or manufacturing capability should have its own page with:

  • A clear name in the terms buyers use, not only your internal model codes.
  • A specification table in HTML, with units and tolerances.
  • Materials, finishes and options.
  • Downloadable datasheets, drawings or CAD files where relevant.
  • A request-a-quote link on the page itself.

2. A factory and quality page

Buyers want to know they are dealing with the manufacturer. Show production equipment, inspection and testing, the quality system and certificates with their scope and validity. Real photos matter more than design.

3. Commercial basics, stated plainly

Many RFQs stall on simple questions. Answer them on the site:

  • Minimum order quantity, or how MOQ is decided.
  • Typical lead time for samples and for production.
  • Sample policy and cost.
  • Incoterms you usually quote and the ports you ship from.
  • Payment terms you normally accept.

4. An inquiry form that asks for the right things

A good RFQ form is short but specific. Useful fields include:

  • Product or part, and quantity.
  • Target date.
  • Drawing or file upload, with an NDA option if you offer one.
  • Application or end use.
  • Contact details and the preferred way to reply — email, WeChat or WhatsApp.

After submission, show a confirmation that says who will reply and when, and send an automatic acknowledgment. Then make sure someone actually replies.

5. Application and industry pages

Engineers often search by problem or application, not by product name. Pages that explain how your product is used in a specific industry — with the relevant standards and examples you are allowed to share — bring better-qualified visitors and give them confidence you understand their field. Our industry pages show the sectors we work with.

6. Technical basics for search

None of this helps if search engines cannot read it:

  • Each page has a unique, descriptive title and one clear heading.
  • Specifications are text, not images.
  • Pages load quickly on mobile, with compressed images.
  • The site has a sitemap, and important pages are linked from the navigation.
  • Google Search Console is connected so you can see how buyers find you.

Our SEO service covers this technical layer.

7. One consistent contact path

Use one company name, one set of contact details and one inquiry process across the website, LinkedIn and marketplaces. Route different requests — quotes, support, partnerships — to the right person, so none of them waits.

Questions

Common questions

Do we need prices on the website?

Not usually for custom or industrial products. But clear MOQ, lead-time and sample information helps buyers decide to send an RFQ.

Should the RFQ form allow file uploads?

For drawing-based work, yes. State what file types you accept and how you protect them.
