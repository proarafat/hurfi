From PDF catalogue to product pages: making your range searchable

Many factories put their whole range in one big PDF catalogue and link to it from the website. Buyers then have to download it, scroll and guess. Search engines can read PDFs, but a catalogue is no substitute for product pages. This guide explains how to turn a catalogue into pages buyers can find and compare.

Can Google read PDFs?

Yes. Google’s list of file types it can index includes Adobe Portable Document Format (.pdf). But one PDF for hundreds of products gives search engines and buyers one long document instead of one clear page per product family.

Why product pages work better

  • Each page can match the searches for that product type.
  • Buyers land directly on the product they need.
  • Specification tables in HTML are easy to read on phones and to copy into comparisons.
  • Each page can carry its own RFQ form, certificates and related products.
  • You can see which products attract inquiries.

Audit the catalogue first

List every product family, model and specification in the catalogue. Note what is out of date, what is missing and which families bring the most valuable inquiries. Start with those.

Group into families, not hundreds of thin pages

A page for every tiny variant often creates many near-duplicate pages. Usually one page per product family or series, with a model table, works better. Give a model its own page only when buyers search for it or it differs significantly.

Build one good template

  • A clear H1 with the product type and key specification.
  • A short introduction: what it is, what it is for, who uses it.
  • A specification table in HTML.
  • Applications and options.
  • Certificates that cover the product.
  • Downloads: datasheet, manual, drawings.
  • An RFQ form and related products.

Keep PDFs as downloads

PDFs remain useful: buyers forward them, print them and file them. Offer a datasheet PDF beside each product page, keep the file names descriptive and make sure the PDF and the page say the same thing.

Write titles that match the product

Google’s guidance on title links asks for descriptive and concise text in each page’s title element. Use the product type and its main specification, not just a model code.

Describe products for search

Where it fits and the facts are accurate, Product structured data can describe your products to Google. Google’s documentation describes product snippets for pages where people cannot directly purchase the product — the situation of most B2B product pages.

A worked example

Imagine a 120-page pump catalogue with 15 series and 400 models. Instead of one PDF, the website gets 15 series pages, each with a model table, curves, materials, certificates and a datasheet download. Category pages group the series by application. The PDF stays available for buyers who want it.

The buyer searching for a specific type of pump now lands on the right series page, compares models in a table and sends an RFQ from that page — and you know which series it came from.

Keep pages and PDFs in step

  • Change specifications in one place first, then update the other.
  • Put a revision date on each datasheet.
  • Remove discontinued models from both, or mark them clearly.
  • Check every quarter that downloads still open.

Link, publish and measure

Link families from your category pages and from each other, add them to your XML sitemap and track which pages lead to inquiries. Add the next batch of families based on what you learn.

Questions

Common questions

Can Google index our PDF catalogue?

Yes, Google can index PDFs. But product pages usually serve buyers and search better than one long catalogue.

Should we remove the PDF?

No. Keep it as a download, and keep it consistent with the product pages.

How many pages do we need?

Usually one per product family or series, with model tables. Start with the families that matter most.

Our catalogue is in Chinese. Can you help?

Yes. We work from Chinese documents and write English pages with your team.

How long does it take?

It depends on the range. We usually start with a template and the top families, then add the rest in batches.

Will this improve our rankings?

It usually helps buyers and search find the right product, but nobody can guarantee rankings.

What about prices?

Most B2B product pages do not show prices; MOQs, lead times and an RFQ form work better.
