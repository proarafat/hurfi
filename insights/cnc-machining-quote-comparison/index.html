How to compare CNC machining quotes

Two CNC suppliers can answer the same RFQ with prices that differ by half and still both be right, because they are quoting different scopes. This guide shows buyers how to control the drawing package, compare tolerance and inspection, and calculate the real landed cost before choosing a machining supplier.

Why CNC machining quotes are hard to compare

A CNC quote is a set of assumptions about drawing revision, material, tolerance, finish, inspection, quantity, packaging, lead time and delivery terms. When those assumptions differ, the headline price is not a like-for-like number.

The lowest quote is often the one with the narrowest scope: stock material instead of certified material, general tolerances instead of inspected critical dimensions, or a lead time that starts only after the material arrives. Make the assumptions visible before comparing price.

Send one controlled drawing package to every supplier

Send a 3D model for geometry and a 2D drawing for tolerances, notes, material, heat treatment and finish. State the drawing revision on every document, and include the quantity, target date, delivery destination and the quality documents required.

ASME Y14.5 is the authoritative guideline for geometric dimensioning and tolerancing, while ISO 2768-1 covers general tolerances when individual tolerances are not indicated [1][2]. Say which convention governs the drawing; mixing them is a common source of dispute.

  • STEP or native CAD model, with the same revision as the drawing.
  • 2D PDF drawing showing datums, tolerances, threads, finishes and notes.
  • Material and alloy specification, temper or heat treatment, and any approved substitutes.
  • Critical dimensions and the inspection method the buyer will accept.
  • Quantity breaks, target date and delivery destination.
  • Required certificates, first-article report and packaging.

Compare tolerance with process capability

A tight tolerance is a process requirement, not a wish. Ask the supplier to confirm the machine, fixture and inspection method for each critical feature, and whether the price includes a CMM report or only a dimensional check.

If the drawing has no individual tolerance, confirm the ISO 2768-1 class in the RFQ. Tightening every tolerance to avoid a conversation raises cost and lead time without improving fit. Tolerance should follow function.

Check material and finish assumptions

Confirm the alloy, temper, mill certificate, heat number, surface finish and coating thickness. Ask whether the material is bought to order or pulled from stock, and require written approval before any substitution.

For regulated products, add traceability, first-article inspection and production-part approval to the scope. A material certificate that does not match the heat number on the parts is not traceability.

Inspection and documentation: what is included?

Ask what inspection is included in the unit price: first article, in-process checks, final dimensional report, thread gauges, surface-roughness checks, material certificates and packing inspection. Agree the sampling level and who pays for third-party inspection.

ISO 9001:2026 is a quality management system standard, not a product certification [3]. Check the certificate’s scope and validity, then verify the actual inspection records for the order. A logo on a website is not evidence that a specific batch was measured.

Separate tooling, setup and quantity breaks

A CNC price can include programming, fixtures, setup, tooling, first-article inspection, material, machining, finishing, inspection and packaging. Ask which costs are one-time and which repeat on every order.

Request prices at realistic quantity bands to see how the setup cost is amortized. Ask who owns custom fixtures, whether they are dedicated to the buyer, and what happens to them if production moves.

Lead time, Incoterms and landed cost

A quoted lead time can mean machining only, machining after material arrives, or delivery after drawing approval. Ask for the production lead time after drawing and first-article approval, and confirm the capacity reserved for the order.

Compare Ex Works, FOB and DAP quotes under Incoterms 2020, then add freight, insurance, duty, clearance, packaging and the cost of the buyer’s own inspection. The lowest ex-works price can be the highest landed cost.

Score quotes instead of ranking by price

Set the weights before seeing prices: scope completeness, tolerance capability, material traceability, inspection, lead time, communication, price and payment terms. Score each quote against the same evidence and keep the assumptions beside the score.

For critical parts, pay for a sample and inspect it against the drawing. A sample proves the process once; production approval still needs a first-article report and change control.

How to approve the first article before production

First-article inspection is the control point between a quotation and a production order. The supplier measures the first parts against the drawing, records the actual values and sends the report with the material certificate and any deviation requests. The buyer approves, rejects or approves with conditions in writing.

Agree the report format before the order: which features are measured, which instrument is used, how many parts are sampled and who signs. For critical features, ask for a layout inspection with actual values rather than a pass or fail tick. Photographs help, but they do not replace dimensional data.

Change control matters as much as the first measurement. A revised drawing needs a new revision number, a new first-article report for the changed features and a clear date from which the new revision applies. Without that discipline, an approved sample can drift into a different production process.

What a CNC supplier should publish

Publish the materials you machine, achievable tolerances, equipment list, inspection equipment, finishing options, certifications, typical lead times and an RFQ form that accepts drawings. Buyers compare capability before they contact a supplier.

Questions

Common questions

What is the difference between ISO 2768 and GD&T?

ISO 2768-1 sets general tolerances for dimensions without individual tolerance indications. GD&T, as defined in ASME Y14.5, communicates design intent through datums, symbols and geometric controls.

Should I send a STEP file or a PDF drawing?

Send both. The STEP file defines geometry; the PDF drawing defines tolerances, notes, material, finish and revision. The drawing controls acceptance.

Why do CNC machining quotes vary so much?

Suppliers make different assumptions about material, tolerance, finish, inspection, quantity, lead time and delivery terms. Compare the scope before comparing the price.

What is a fair MOQ for CNC machining?

There is no universal MOQ. Setup and programming dominate small lots, so ask for the quantity break where the unit price stabilises and check whether material has a minimum order.

How do I check a supplier’s tolerance capability?

Ask for a capability study or a sample part measured on the critical features, and confirm the machine, fixture and inspection method used. A certificate alone does not prove capability.

What should a CNC machining quote include?

It should include the drawing revision, material and certificate, operations, tolerance and inspection, finish, quantity, unit price, setup or tooling, lead-time basis, packaging, Incoterm and payment terms.
