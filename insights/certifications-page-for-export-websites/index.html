The certifications page: how exporters should present certificates to overseas buyers

For many overseas buyers, certifications are the first filter: no right certificate, no RFQ. Yet many factory websites show a wall of logos with no detail. This guide explains how to build a certifications page that buyers trust, and how to link it to your products.

Why certifications decide the first call

A buyer in Europe, North America or the Middle East usually has to prove that what they import meets local rules. Before contacting you, they want to know whether your products already comply. If your site does not say, the easiest decision is to move on to a supplier whose site does.

What the EU expects from manufacturers

The European Commission’s CE marking page explains that manufacturers are responsible for carrying out the conformity assessment, setting up the technical file, issuing the EU declaration of conformity and affixing the CE marking. Importers and distributors must make sure the products they place on the market meet the rules.

This is why European buyers ask for your declaration of conformity and test reports: their own responsibility depends on yours.

Group certificates by market

A list of logos is hard to use. Group your certificates by the market or buyer they matter to:

  • European Union: CE-related declarations and test reports, and other EU requirements that apply to your products.
  • Other markets you sell to: the certificates or registrations each requires, as confirmed by the relevant authority.
  • Quality and management systems: ISO or industry-specific certificates.
  • China: compulsory certification (CCC) and other domestic approvals where relevant.

Give the details buyers check

  • Certificate name and number.
  • Issuing body or test laboratory.
  • Scope: which products and models it covers.
  • Issue and expiry dates.
  • A downloadable copy or the option to request one.

Never overclaim

Only list certificates you hold, for the products they really cover. Do not imply that a quality-system certificate is a product certificate, or that a test report is a certification. A buyer who finds one overstatement will doubt everything else — and in regulated markets it can create legal problems for both of you.

Link certificates to products

On every product page, say which certificates cover that product and link to the certifications page. On the certifications page, link back to the products. Buyers then see compliance in context, and search engines see clear relationships between pages.

Where it fits the page and the information is accurate, Product structured data can describe your products to Google.

Keep it current

Certificates expire, scopes change and new models need testing. Give one person the job of checking the page every quarter. An expired certificate on your website tells a buyer you are not paying attention.

Make documents easy to request

Some documents you may not want to publish openly. Offer them on request with a short form: the buyer’s company, market and products of interest. That request is itself a strong sales lead.

A simple layout that works

Most manufacturers can use the same structure: a short introduction, then one block per market, then quality-system certificates, then a “request documents” form. Inside each market block, a table works well:

  • Column 1: certificate or document name.
  • Column 2: products and models covered.
  • Column 3: issuing body or laboratory.
  • Column 4: valid until.
  • Column 5: download or request link.

Keep the table in HTML, not as an image, so buyers can search it and read it on a phone.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Logos of certification marks the company does not hold.
  • Certificates issued to a different company, such as a trading partner, without saying so.
  • Expired certificates still shown as current.
  • Test reports presented as certifications.
  • Scanned certificates so blurred that the number cannot be read.

Write it for people, not only for search

Google’s guidance on helpful, reliable, people-first content applies here too. A clear page that answers a buyer’s compliance questions is more useful — and more convincing — than a page of keywords.

Questions

Common questions

Should we show certificate logos?

You can, but always with the details: number, issuer, scope and validity. Logos alone do not answer buyers’ questions.

Who is responsible for CE marking?

The European Commission explains that the manufacturer carries out the conformity assessment, prepares the technical file, issues the declaration of conformity and affixes the CE marking.

Can we publish test reports?

You can publish them or offer them on request. Make clear which products and models they cover.

We hold ISO 9001. Is that a product certificate?

No. It certifies a quality-management system. Present it as that, separately from product certifications.

Do we need a page per certificate?

Usually one certifications page grouped by market is enough, plus the relevant certificates on each product page.

How often should we update it?

At least every quarter, and whenever a certificate is issued, renewed or expires.

Can Hurfi tell us which certificates we need?

No. That is for your compliance team, test laboratories or authorities. We help you present what you hold clearly and accurately.
