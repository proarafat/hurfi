The shipping and logistics page: answering buyers’ delivery questions before they ask

“Which port? Which terms? How long?” Almost every overseas buyer asks these questions, often before asking about price. A clear shipping and logistics page answers them once, builds confidence and saves your sales team many emails. This guide explains what to include.

Why buyers care so much

A buyer has to land your product at their door at a known cost and on a known date. If they cannot work that out, they cannot compare you with other suppliers. A shipping page turns an unknown into something they can plan.

Use Incoterms correctly

The International Chamber of Commerce describes the Incoterms® rules as eleven three-letter trade terms that clarify the tasks, costs and risks involved in delivering goods from sellers to buyers. Say which terms you usually offer and name the version you use, for example Incoterms® 2020.

Do not list every term if you only quote two or three. Be clear about what you will and will not arrange.

Name your ports and routes

Tell buyers where goods leave China. Many manufacturers ship through large container ports — Jiaxing’s port, for example, handled 4.255 million TEU in 2025 and Fujian’s ports 18.4364 million TEU. Inland suppliers should explain how goods reach the coast or travel by rail.

  • Main port(s) of loading.
  • Rail or river routes if you use them.
  • Air freight for samples or urgent orders.
  • Typical transit times to your main markets, as ranges.

Explain packing

  • Standard export packing for each product family.
  • Pallet, crate or carton options and dimensions.
  • Container loading: how many units per 20′ or 40′ container, if useful.
  • Marking, labelling and documents that travel with the goods.

Give realistic lead times

Give ranges for standard products, custom products and repeat orders, and say what affects them: materials, tooling, testing or season. Buyers trust realistic ranges more than promises that turn out to be wrong.

Samples and small orders

Many relationships start with a sample. Explain how buyers can get one, what it costs, how it is shipped and how long it takes. If you accept small trial orders, say so and give the minimum.

Documents and special cargo

List the export documents you routinely supply, and explain how you handle special cases such as oversized machinery or goods that need dangerous-goods handling. Do not claim capabilities you do not have; say instead which forwarders or partners you work with.

Link it from every product page

Put a short “Shipping and lead times” line on each product page with a link to the full page. Add the shipping page to your main navigation or footer and to your XML sitemap. A buyer should never have to search for it.

A checklist for the page

  • Ports of loading and any inland routes.
  • Incoterms you usually quote, with the version.
  • Packing by product family, with photos.
  • Lead-time ranges for samples, standard and custom orders.
  • Documents you supply with each shipment.
  • Who to contact for a freight quote.

If you can tick every item, most buyers will have the information they need to compare you and ask for a quote.

Show it, do not just say it

Photos and short videos of packing, loading and containers leaving your factory are some of the most convincing content an exporter can publish. They show care, capacity and that you really ship — things buyers cannot judge from text alone.

Keep it honest and current

Freight rates and transit times change. Avoid quoting fixed prices; give the information that stays stable — ports, terms, packing and ranges — and invite buyers to request a current quote.

Questions

Common questions

What are Incoterms?

The ICC describes them as eleven three-letter trade terms that clarify the tasks, costs and risks in delivering goods from seller to buyer.

Should we publish freight prices?

Usually not. Rates change. Publish ports, terms, packing and lead-time ranges, and quote freight on request.

We are an inland supplier. What should we say?

Explain how goods reach the port or travel by rail, and give realistic transit times.

Should the shipping page list every Incoterm?

No. List the terms you usually offer and name the version.

Do buyers care about packing?

Yes, especially for machinery, fragile goods and long routes. Photos of real packing help.

Where should the page appear?

In the navigation or footer, linked from every product page, and in your sitemap.

Can Hurfi write this page for us?

Yes. We draft it from your team’s information and your logistics staff approve every detail.
