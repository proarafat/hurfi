Canton Fair guide for buyers and exporters

The 140th Canton Fair runs in three phases from 15 October to 4 November 2026 in Guangzhou. This guide gives the confirmed dates, which phase covers which products, how buyers register, and what a manufacturer should fix on its website before the fair and after it.

What the Canton Fair is

The China Import and Export Fair, held in Guangzhou and known as the Canton Fair, has run every spring and autumn since 1957. It is a trade fair: buyers and exhibitors register, and it is not open to the general public. Each session runs in three phases of about five days, with two- to three-day intervals between them to reset the halls.

The scale is unusual. The organiser lists 1.55 million square metres of exhibition area and more than 32,000 exhibitors, and reports 310,000 overseas buyers from 223 countries and regions at the 138th session. For a manufacturer, that means a buyer you meet at one booth is meeting dozens of suppliers in the same week.

2026 dates, hours and venue

The organiser has published both 2026 sessions. All phases run at the China Import and Export Fair Complex, No. 382 Yuejiang Zhong Road, Guangzhou, from 9:30 to 18:00.

  • 139th (Spring 2026): Phase 1, 15–19 April; Phase 2, 23–27 April; Phase 3, 1–5 May.
  • 140th (Autumn 2026): Phase 1, 15–19 October; Phase 2, 23–27 October; Phase 3, 31 October–4 November.
  • Intervals in autumn 2026: 20–22 October and 28–30 October, used to dismantle and rebuild the halls.
  • Hosts and organiser: the Ministry of Commerce and the Guangdong provincial government host; the China Foreign Trade Centre organises.

Which phase are your products in?

Phases are grouped by product, so buyers plan travel around the phase that matches what they source. The organiser's list is long; this is the summary most buyers use.

  • Phase 1 — industrial and technical: household appliances, consumer electronics and information products, industrial automation, processing, power and general machinery, construction and agricultural machinery, new materials and chemicals, vehicles and spare parts, motorcycles, bicycles, lighting, hardware and tools, new energy resources.
  • Phase 2 — consumer and building: ceramics, kitchenware and household items, glass and art ceramics, home decorations, gardening, festival products, gifts, clocks and optical instruments, building and decorative materials, sanitary and bathroom equipment, furniture.
  • Phase 3 — fashion, textiles and health: clothing and accessories, underwear, sportswear, furs and leather, textile raw materials and fabrics, shoes, cases and bags, home textiles and carpets, office supplies, food, medicines, health products and medical devices, personal care, sports and recreation, pet products, toys and children's items.

If you are buying: registration and the invitation letter

Buyer registration is handled by the organiser, through its buyer platform. You register with passport and company details, receive a buyer badge, and use the same system for the visa invitation letter many first-time visitors need.

  • Register early: badge approval and visa timing are the two things that most often go wrong close to a session.
  • Apply for the invitation letter only through the organiser's buyer system; treat unsolicited offers of letters or badges as suspect.
  • Plan halls by phase before you book flights — arriving in the wrong phase means the suppliers you want are not there.
  • Book accommodation early; the Pazhou area fills for the weeks around each phase.
  • Bring your own product specifications and target prices, so booth conversations start at the technical level.

If you are exhibiting: fix your website first

Buyers use the fair to build a shortlist, then go online to cut it down. Everything you claim at the booth should already be on your site, in text a search engine can read, because that is what the buyer checks in the hotel that evening.

  • A page for every product you exhibit, with specifications in HTML rather than only inside a PDF catalogue.
  • Certificates and approvals on the product pages they cover, with scope and market.
  • MOQ, lead time and packing stated plainly, so the first email is about the project and not about basics.
  • A fair page with booth number, hall and phase, linking to the products on show.
  • A QR code on brochures and booth panels that opens the exact product section, tagged so you can see visits that came from the fair.
  • An English enquiry form that works on a phone, plus a named contact with a direct email.

Follow-up decides who gets the RFQ

Most exhibitors collect more cards than they can follow up well. The supplier that replies within a few days, answers the buyer's actual question and links to the right product page usually wins the quotation request, even against a cheaper competitor.

Our guide to trade show follow-up for manufacturers covers the email structure and a 30-day plan; industrial product page SEO covers the pages the email should point to.

Planning the 2027 sessions

The spring session normally falls in mid-April with the same three-phase pattern, and the autumn session in mid-October. Exact dates, hall layout and registration windows are published by the organiser, and they are the only source worth planning around; third-party date lists are often copied and wrong.

A checklist for this session

  • Confirm which phase your products are in, and book travel for those days only.
  • Publish or refresh the product pages for everything on the booth.
  • Add the fair page with booth, hall and phase, and link it from the home page.
  • Create one tracked link or QR code for each product group.
  • Decide who replies to enquiries, and in what language, before the first day.
  • Log every contact with product interest and market, not just a name.
  • Send the first follow-up within three days of meeting the buyer.
  • Review which page the fair traffic landed on, and fix what it exposed.

Common questions

When is the next Canton Fair?

The 140th session (autumn 2026) runs 15–19 October, 23–27 October and 31 October–4 November 2026 in Guangzhou. The 139th ran 15 April–5 May 2026 in the same three-phase pattern.

Is the Canton Fair open to the public?

No. It is a trade fair for registered buyers and exhibitors. Visitors register through the organiser and are issued a buyer badge.

How do I register as an overseas buyer?

Through the organiser's buyer platform, with passport and company details. Register well before the session so the badge and any visa paperwork arrive in time.

How do I get a Canton Fair invitation letter for a visa?

Request it through the organiser's buyer system after registering. Treat any paid or unsolicited offer of an invitation letter as suspect.

Which phase should I attend?

The one that matches what you source: Phase 1 for machinery, electronics, vehicles, hardware and building materials; Phase 2 for houseware, gifts and furniture; Phase 3 for textiles, clothing, shoes, food and health products.

How large is the fair?

The organiser lists 1.55 million square metres and more than 32,000 exhibitors. The 138th session reported 310,000 overseas buyers from 223 countries and regions.

Do we need a Chinese-speaking team at the fair?

Most export booths work in English. A Mandarin speaker still helps with smaller suppliers, local transport and contracts, but it is not a requirement for buyer meetings.

How should an exporter use the fair for SEO?

Publish a fair page with booth details, link it to the exhibited products, tag the QR links, and keep the product pages updated afterwards so the traffic the fair sends can convert.

Can Hurfi help us prepare?

Yes. We can build or refresh the product pages, write the fair page, set up tracked links and prepare the follow-up templates before the session.
