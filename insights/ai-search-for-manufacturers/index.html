AI search for manufacturers: what actually matters

Buyers increasingly see AI-generated summaries in Google before they see a list of links. Manufacturers ask whether they need special “AI optimisation”. Google’s own documentation gives a clear answer, and it is less dramatic than many sales pitches suggest.

What Google says about AI features

Google’s page on AI features and your website states that there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and that no special schema.org structured data is needed. The same SEO foundations apply: pages must be indexable and eligible to be shown with a snippet.

In other words, a manufacturer that does normal SEO well is already doing most of what is needed.

Write pages that answer real buyer questions

AI summaries draw on pages that answer a question clearly. For a manufacturer, those questions are specific: what tolerance can you hold, which standard does this cable meet, what is the MOQ for custom packaging. Pages that answer them in plain text, with numbers and conditions, are useful to buyers and to AI systems alike.

Google’s 2025 post on succeeding in AI search gives the same advice: focus on making unique, non-commodity content that visitors will find helpful and satisfying.

  • Specifications in HTML text, not only images or PDFs
  • FAQs that match questions your sales team actually receives
  • Application pages that explain where and why a product is used

Be clear about who you are

Summaries and buyers both benefit when the website makes the company clear: legal name, factory address, what you make yourself, certificates with scope, and who to contact. Inconsistent names or addresses across your site, LinkedIn and marketplace listings create doubt.

Use structured data for accuracy, not tricks

Structured data such as Organization, Product and FAQ markup helps search engines understand a page, but Google says it is not required for AI features. Use it to describe what is visibly on the page, accurately. Markup that does not match the page can cause problems rather than solve them.

Avoid the hype

  • Services promising guaranteed placement in AI answers
  • Mass-generated pages written only for AI crawlers
  • Hidden text or keyword lists aimed at AI systems
  • Claims that a special file or markup is required to appear

Measure what reaches you

Google says traffic from AI features is included in Search Console’s Performance report, within the “Web” search type. What matters for a manufacturer is still the same: qualified inquiries from the right buyers. Track RFQs by landing page so you can see which content works.

What buyers ask AI assistants

Buyers increasingly start supplier research by asking an AI assistant a long question — for example, “Which manufacturers make IP67 LED drivers with UL certification and a five-year warranty?” The answers these tools give draw on pages they can find and understand.

You cannot control which supplier an AI tool names. You can make sure your pages contain clear, specific answers to the questions buyers actually ask — specifications, certifications, minimum order quantities, lead times — so that they are good candidates to be found and cited. Google’s guidance for its AI features says the same SEO best practices apply as for search in general.

A practical checklist for your top pages

Start with the ten pages that matter most for new buyers: your key product families, your capability page and your about page.

  • Specifications are in HTML text, not only in images or PDFs.
  • Certifications are named exactly, with the scope they cover.
  • The page states who you are: company name, location, factory or trading company.
  • Common questions are answered on the page in plain language.
  • The page is crawlable: no login, no blocking in robots.txt, and it is linked from other pages.
  • Structured data, where used, matches what is visible on the page.

Keep facts consistent everywhere

AI tools and search engines compare information across sources. If your website says one capacity, your marketplace store another and your LinkedIn page a third, buyers — and machines — cannot tell which is right.

Pick one set of facts — company name, address, year founded, certifications, capacities — and use it on your website, marketplace profiles, LinkedIn and trade-fair listings. Update all of them when something changes.

How to tell whether it is working

There is no separate report for every AI tool. Google says traffic from its AI features is included in Search Console’s Performance report, under the “Web” search type. Other assistants may appear as referral traffic in your analytics.

Watch three things over a few months: impressions and clicks for your key product pages in Search Console, referral visits from AI assistants in your analytics, and — most important — whether new inquiries mention finding you through search or an AI tool. Ask new contacts how they found you, and record the answer.

Questions

Common questions

Do we need special optimisation for Google’s AI Overviews?

Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode beyond normal SEO best practices.

Do we need special structured data for AI search?

No. Google states that no special schema.org structured data is needed. Use structured data to describe your pages accurately.

Will AI search reduce our website traffic?

It may change how buyers reach you. Pages that answer specific technical questions remain valuable, and buyers still visit suppliers’ sites before sending an RFQ.

Should we write content for AI systems instead of people?

No. Write for buyers. Google’s guidance focuses on helpful, people-first content.

Can an agency guarantee we appear in AI answers?

No one can guarantee that. Be wary of anyone who does.

What content works best for manufacturers?

Specific product, specification, application and FAQ pages that answer real buyer questions with numbers and conditions.

How do we see AI traffic?

It is included in Search Console performance reports. Track inquiries by landing page in analytics.

Does consistency across platforms matter?

Yes. Use the same company name, address and product names on your website, LinkedIn and listings.

Can Hurfi help?

Yes. Our SEO work covers the content, technical and structured-data basics that both classic and AI search rely on.
