Product page SEO for industrial manufacturers

For a manufacturer, the product page does most of the selling. It is where an engineer decides whether to send an RFQ, and where Google decides whether the page answers a search. This guide covers what a good industrial product page contains.

Start with a descriptive title

The title should say what the product is in the buyer’s words, with the most important specification or variant: for example “Stainless steel ball valve, DN15–DN100, PN40” rather than “Product 3”. Google’s SEO Starter Guide recommends descriptive titles and notes that Google does not use the keywords meta tag.

Put specifications in HTML text

Engineers search for numbers: sizes, ratings, tolerances, materials, standards. If those numbers are only in an image or a PDF, they are harder for buyers to scan and for search engines to read. Publish a specification table on the page and keep the PDF as a download.

  • Units and test conditions next to every value
  • Variants in one table rather than separate thin pages
  • Materials and finishes with the standards they meet
  • Drawings or dimension diagrams you own

Explain applications and limits

Say where the product is used and where it should not be used. Application text helps buyers who search by problem rather than by product name, and honest limits build trust with engineers.

Be exact about compliance

List certificates for the product and market, with the holder and scope. For products sold in the EU that need CE marking, the European Commission explains that the manufacturer carries out the conformity assessment, sets up the technical file, issues the EU declaration of conformity and affixes the marking. Only claim what you hold.

Use structured data that matches the page

Google’s Product structured data documentation describes two types: Product snippets for pages that are not purchase pages, and merchant listings for pages where a product can be bought. Most B2B product pages fit the first. Mark up only what is visible on the page.

End with an RFQ path

Every product page should end with a short form or a clear contact: what the buyer needs (quantity, specification, market, date) and who will answer. Track submissions by page so you know which products bring inquiries.

A product page outline you can reuse

Most industrial product pages work well with the same basic outline. It gives buyers what they need in the order they look for it, and gives search engines clear text to work with.

  1. Title and one-line summary: what the product is, its key specification and who it is for.
  2. Key specifications table: in HTML, with units, tolerances and test conditions.
  3. Applications: where it is used, and where it should not be used.
  4. Materials, options and customisation: what can be changed and what cannot.
  5. Compliance and documents: certificates, test reports and datasheets, with their scope.
  6. Packaging, MOQ and lead time: typical ranges, clearly stated as such.
  7. RFQ path: a short form or contact link with what information to include.

How to handle hundreds of variants

Industrial products often come in many sizes, materials and ratings. Creating a page for every variant usually produces hundreds of near-identical pages that do not help buyers and may be treated as low value.

A better approach is one strong page per product family, with a variant table and filters. Create separate pages only for variants that buyers search for by name, or that have different applications, certifications or documents. Link variant pages back to the family page, and make sure each one has something unique to say.

Common mistakes on factory product pages

  • Specifications only in images or PDFs, so they cannot be searched or copied.
  • Titles that are only a model number, with no description of the product.
  • Copying a competitor’s or a marketplace description word for word.
  • Vague claims such as “high quality” and “best price” instead of measured values.
  • CE or other marks shown without saying which directives or standards apply.
  • No visible way to ask a question or request a quote.

Who should review a product page

Product pages carry technical and legal weight. A wrong tolerance or an overstated certification can cost an order or create liability. Before a page is published, an engineer should check the specifications and applications, someone responsible for quality should check compliance statements, and a sales person should check that the page answers the questions buyers ask most.

Record who reviewed each page and when. When a specification or certificate changes, update the page and the date, so buyers and search engines see current information.

Questions

Common questions

What makes an industrial product page rank?

Clear relevance to the search — a descriptive title, specifications in text, application content — plus a site that is crawlable, fast and trusted.

Should every variant have its own page?

Usually not. Group minor variants in a table on one page. Give separate pages to products that buyers search for separately.

Are PDF datasheets bad for SEO?

Not bad, but not enough. Put the key specifications on the HTML page and offer the PDF too.

Does Google use the keywords meta tag?

No. Google states that it does not use the keywords meta tag.

Which structured data should B2B product pages use?

Usually Product snippets markup, which Google describes for non-purchase pages. Mark up only what is visible.

Can we list CE or other certificates?

Yes, if you hold them for that product. Name the product, scope and holder. The manufacturer is responsible for CE conformity assessment.

Should we show prices?

For configured or custom products, often not. Explain what affects price and invite an RFQ.

How long should a product page be?

As long as it needs to answer the buyer’s questions. Specifications, applications, compliance and an RFQ form usually take more than a paragraph.

Can Hurfi write our product pages?

Yes. We draft them from your documents with your engineers and you approve every technical statement.
