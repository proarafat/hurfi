Solar and renewable power in 2025: the official and industry figures

From China’s national statistics and the machinery and electronics export chamber (citing customs data): output and installations kept growing while module export values fell.

  • China made about 832.7 GW of solar cells in 2025, up 7.6%; output of ultra-clear glass for solar use was about 3.16 billion square metres, up 3.4%.[1]
  • Installed solar capacity reached 1,201.73 GW at the end of 2025, up 35.4%, and wind capacity 640.01 GW, up 22.9%. Solar plants generated 1,173.24 TWh, up 39.8%.[1]
  • Exports of wafers, cells and modules together were US$29.45 billion, down 8%. Module exports were US$23.7 billion (−15.3%) for 249.8 GW (+5.7%).[2]
  • Cell exports rose to US$4.5 billion (+72.6%) for 111.2 GW (+90.6%). India and Indonesia took 40% and 27% of them.[2]
  • Inverter exports were US$9.04 billion (+9.4%), with the Netherlands, Germany, Australia, Brazil and India as the main markets.[2]

Volume is not the problem; differentiation is. When dozens of factories offer the same wattage, buyers decide on documentation, bankability evidence and local support, and a website can show all three.

What overseas buyers compare in solar and renewable power

Solar buyers compare a short list of suppliers within one product family:

Modules

Cell technology, power classes, bifacial gain claims, degradation warranty and the test reports behind them. Distributors also compare pallet quantities and lead times.

Inverters and power electronics

String, hybrid and micro inverters, optimisers and combiner boxes: efficiency curves, grid codes supported, monitoring platform and firmware update policy.

Mounting, tracking and racking

Load calculations, corrosion protection, installation time and structural reports.

Balance of system

Cables, connectors, junction boxes and isolators: ratings, certificates and compatibility.

Wind components

Bearings, gearboxes, blade parts and condition monitoring, sold to turbine makers and service firms who ask for material traceability and test records.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Requirements depend on product and market. These references come up most often:

  • IEC 61215-1:2021 sets design qualification and type approval tests for terrestrial PV modules intended for long-term open-air use, including crystalline silicon and thin-film modules, and adds instructions for bifacial modules. The IEC notes that test results are not a prediction of module lifetime.[3]
  • In the EU, electrical equipment rated between 50 and 1000 V AC or 75 and 1500 V DC falls under the Low Voltage Directive (2014/35/EU).[4]
  • The EMC Directive requires electrical and electronic equipment not to generate, or be affected by, electromagnetic disturbance, which matters for inverters and power electronics.[5]
  • Google’s documentation on product structured data explains how product pages can carry machine-readable details such as name, image and offers, which helps search engines understand a module or inverter page.[6]

We do not tell buyers which certificates you hold; you do. The website’s job is to show each certificate with its scope, model numbers, issuing body and date, so a buyer’s engineer can check it in seconds.

Questions overseas buyers ask solar and renewable power suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Which test reports cover this exact model?

Link each datasheet to the report numbers and the lab that issued them, and say which power classes are covered.

Are you on our approved-vendor or bankability lists?

If you are, say which lists and when. If not, say what independent testing and factory audits you can share instead.

What warranty do you give, and who honours it here?

Product and performance warranty terms, the claims process and whether there is a local service partner.

How do your inverters connect to our grid?

Grid codes supported per country, monitoring options and how firmware updates are delivered.

Can you supply a full container and what are the lead times?

Pallet and container quantities, typical production and shipping lead times and the ports you ship from.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for solar and renewable power manufacturers

The order we usually work in. Most factories already have the material; it is just not online in a usable form.

  1. Split the site by product family and buyer: modules, inverters, mounting, components. Each family gets its own landing page and its own RFQ form fields.
  2. Publish datasheets as HTML tables as well as PDFs, with power classes, efficiencies and dimensions that search engines and buyers can read.
  3. Build a certificates page that lists each certificate with model coverage, issuing body, report number and validity, and link to it from every product page.
  4. Add project references by country and application (rooftop, commercial, utility) with photos you are allowed to publish and the products used.
  5. Write pages for the comparisons buyers search: bifacial vs monofacial gain, string vs micro inverters, tracker vs fixed tilt, and be honest about where each fits.
  6. Create market pages for the regions you actually ship to, covering grid codes, voltages and the documents importers there ask for.
  7. Measure with Search Console and analytics which product pages bring RFQs, and expand the pages that do.

Questions

Questions from solar and renewable power manufacturers

Can Hurfi help us compete when module prices are falling?

We cannot change prices. We can make your differences visible, so buyers compare more than price per watt.

We sell modules and inverters. Should they share one website?

Usually one site with separate sections, each with its own landing pages, datasheets and RFQ fields.

Should datasheets be PDFs or web pages?

Both: keep the PDF, but put key specifications on the page as text or tables.

How do we present certificates without overstating them?

List each certificate with its scope, model numbers, issuing body and validity date, and link to the certificate or verification page. Do not imply a certificate covers models it does not.

Do you write technical content for solar products?

Yes, from your datasheets, test reports and engineers; your team approves every technical statement.

Can you guarantee first-page rankings for “solar panel manufacturer”?

No one can guarantee rankings. We focus on specific model, specification and comparison searches where serious buyers are, and report results from your own Search Console.

No. Hurfi’s offices are in Xi’an and Bogura, and we work with manufacturers across China remotely and by video call.
