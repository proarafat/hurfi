Environment and circular economy in 2025: the figures

China’s statistics show progress on emissions and water quality; EU figures show the scale of waste still to be handled.

  • China’s CO₂ emissions per unit of GDP fell 5.0% in 2025, and clean energy made up 30.4% of energy consumption, up 1.8 points.[1]
  • 91.4% of China’s national surface-water sections were rated good, and 72.6% of monitored cities met air-quality standards.[1]
  • Recycled-resource logistics value grew 13.2% in 2025, according to the CFLP.[2]
  • In the EU, 14.4 million tonnes of electrical and electronic equipment are placed on the market and 5 million tonnes of e-waste were collected in 2022, 11.2 kg per person.[3]
  • Jiaxing’s energy-saving and environmental manufacturing output was 48.749 billion yuan, up 1.5%.[4]

The gap between waste generated and waste recovered is where equipment suppliers fit. Buyers want proven recovery rates and reliable operation, documented.

What overseas buyers compare in environmental and circular economy

Environmental buyers compare suppliers by waste stream and process:

Recycling and sorting

Shredders, separators and optical sorters: throughput, purity and recovery.

E-waste and battery recycling

Processes, recovery rates, safety and permits.

Waste-to-energy and biological treatment

Capacity, emissions and residues.

Emissions control and monitoring

Filters, scrubbers and continuous monitoring systems.

Wastewater and sludge

Treatment and resource recovery.

Plastics recycling

Washing, extrusion and pelletising lines.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

EU rules create the demand and set the requirements:

  • The recast WEEE Directive, in force since 13 August 2012, aims to protect the environment and health through the collection and recycling of electrical and electronic waste.[3]
  • The revised Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive promotes water reuse, resource recovery from wastewater and energy-neutral treatment plants.[5]
  • The EU packaging regulation aims to make all packaging recyclable in an economically viable way by 2030 and to increase recycled plastics in packaging.[6]
  • The Digital Product Passport will give product data to recyclers and others along the value chain; the Commission lists textiles, tyres and aluminium for 2027 and furniture for 2028.[7]

Say which permits, approvals or certifications your equipment and plants hold, and give recovery and emissions data with conditions.

Questions overseas buyers ask environmental and circular economy suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

What recovery rate and purity will we get?

Performance data with feed conditions.

What are the emissions and residues?

Measured values and how they were tested.

Where are your reference plants?

By waste stream and country.

What are the operating costs?

Energy, consumables and labour.

Can you design a complete plant?

Engineering and integration capability.

Who services it locally?

Service and parts arrangements.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for environmental and circular economy manufacturers

Environmental equipment buyers include operators, municipalities and EPC firms. They need data and references. Our usual order:

  1. Organise by waste stream and by process.
  2. Give each machine or system a page with capacities, performance and operating data.
  3. Publish case studies with feed, output and results, where permitted.
  4. Build a page on compliance and permits per market.
  5. Write pages for the regulation-driven questions buyers search, with links to official sources.
  6. Use an RFQ form that asks for waste type, volume, targets and site details.
  7. Track which waste streams and markets bring inquiries.

Questions from environmental and circular economy manufacturers

Should we write about EU environmental rules?

Yes, factually and with links to official sources, without giving legal advice.

How do we present performance data?

With conditions, test methods and dates, not best-case figures alone.

Can we name reference plants?

With the operator’s permission.

Do municipal buyers search online?

Consultants and engineers who advise them do, and tenders often follow their research.

Can you write technical environmental content?

Yes, from your engineers’ material; they approve all claims.
