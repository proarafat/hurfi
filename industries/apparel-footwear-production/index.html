Apparel and footwear in 2025: the official figures

China’s national statistics show that garment and footwear exports fell in 2025, while provincial output held up. City bulletins show where the work is concentrated.

  • Garment and clothing-accessory exports were 1,082.2 billion yuan in 2025, down 4.4%.[1]
  • China exported 9 billion pairs of footwear (−2.0%) worth 297.5 billion yuan (−10.8%).[1]
  • Fujian province made 5.815 billion garments in 2025, up 0.3%.[2]
  • Wenzhou’s footwear exports were 26.31 billion yuan (−7.3%) and clothing exports 14.31 billion yuan (−5.5%).[3]
  • Taizhou in Zhejiang made 256.034 million pairs of shoes, and Fuzhou 54.4095 million garments.[4][5]

Falling export values and steady volumes mean buyers have choice. Factories that show what they do well (product categories, materials, compliance, speed) are easier to choose than those with a generic “OEM garment factory” page.

What overseas buyers compare in apparel and footwear

Sourcing teams compare factories by category and capability:

Knitwear and jersey

T-shirts, fleece and activewear: fabrics, printing, MOQ per style and colour.

Wovens and outerwear

Shirts, trousers and jackets: construction, trims, seam sealing and technical fabrics.

Sportswear and performance

Functional fabrics, bonding and testing for performance claims.

Workwear and uniforms

Durability, reflective and protective features, and programme supply.

Footwear

Athletic, casual and safety footwear: constructions, materials, lasts and testing.

Bags and accessories

Materials, hardware, branding options and packing.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Compliance depends on product and market. These are the references European buyers raise most often:

  • The EU Textile Labelling Regulation (EU) 1007/2011 requires fibre-composition labelling of textile products; it does not cover size, origin or care labelling.[6]
  • REACH (EC 1907/2006) restricts chemicals of concern; the authorisation process aims to replace substances of very high concern where alternatives exist.[7]
  • OEKO-TEX® STANDARD 100 tests every component, including threads and buttons, against more than 1,000 substances.[8]
  • The Commission’s Digital Product Passport timeline lists textiles for an ecodesign delegated act in 2027, which will change what product data brands ask suppliers for.[9]

List certificates and audits with their scope and validity. If an audit is a client’s own, say so, and never imply a certificate covers products it does not.

Questions overseas buyers ask apparel and footwear suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Which categories do you specialise in?

Show your strongest categories with examples, not every product you could make.

What are your MOQs and sampling lead times?

Per style and colour, with sample and bulk lead times.

Which compliance documents can you provide?

Test reports, certificates and audits with scope and dates.

Do you source fabrics and trims, or do we?

Explain FOB, CMT and full-package options.

What capacity do you have?

Lines, monthly output per category and peak seasons.

Can you supply recycled or traceable materials?

Say what you can document, and how.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for apparel and footwear manufacturers

Apparel sourcing is visual and fast. The website must show capability quickly and handle sample requests well. Our usual order:

  1. Build category pages around what you make best, with photos of real production and finished goods you are allowed to show.
  2. State MOQs, lead times and service models (CMT, FOB, full package) clearly.
  3. Publish a compliance page with certificates, test capabilities and audit summaries.
  4. Show the factory: lines, equipment, quality control and capacity.
  5. Add a sample-request form that captures category, quantity, target price and timeline.
  6. Write pages for specific searches such as a garment type plus a fabric or a certification.
  7. Plan content around buying calendars and trade fairs, and track which categories bring requests.

Questions

Questions from apparel and footwear manufacturers

Can we show the brands we produce for?

Only with written permission. Otherwise describe the category, market and volume.

Should we publish MOQs?

Yes, as ranges. It filters out unsuitable requests and saves time for both sides.

How do we compete with factories in Vietnam or Bangladesh?

By showing what you do better, such as speed, technical capability, materials or flexibility, with evidence.

Do sourcing managers use Google?

Many do, alongside LinkedIn, trade fairs and agents. They also check a factory online after meeting it.

Can you help after trade fairs?

Yes: fair landing pages and follow-up content so new contacts find the right information.

Will you guarantee orders?

No. We make your factory easier to find and evaluate, and report actual inquiries.

Is Hurfi located in Fujian or Zhejiang?

No. Hurfi’s offices are in Xi’an and Bogura; we work with factories anywhere in China remotely.
