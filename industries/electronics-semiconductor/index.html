Export marketing for Chinese electronics and semiconductor manufacturers

Engineers and component buyers abroad check datasheets, compliance and supply terms before they send an RFQ. Hurfi helps Chinese electronics and semiconductor manufacturers make that information easy to find, read and act on.

Book a Consultation →

What overseas buyers check before they contact a electronics and semiconductor supplier

  • Datasheets with part numbers, electrical characteristics, package options and revision history.
  • Compliance documents such as RoHS and REACH declarations; for automotive-grade parts, the AEC-Q qualification only where it has been completed.
  • For PCB and PCBA work: layer counts, materials, minimum trace/space, assembly capability and the workmanship standard you build to (for example IPC-A-610 class).
  • MOQ, sample policy, typical lead times and how long a part stays in production.
  • Traceability: lot tracking, test records and what a buyer receives with each shipment.

What the website and search work should include

  • A parametric product catalog in HTML, with filters that match how engineers search (package, voltage, interface).
  • Capability pages for contract manufacturing services, separate from component product pages, so each answers one kind of buyer.
  • A clear RFQ path that accepts BOMs or Gerber files and states what happens after upload.
  • Search work on part-level and specification queries, plus pages that answer cross-reference and replacement questions you can genuinely support.
  • LinkedIn content that shows test, inspection and production steps rather than generic factory photos.

Gaps we often see

  • Datasheets uploaded as image scans, so the numbers cannot be searched or copied.
  • Certificates mentioned in text but not shown, or shown without the scope and validity date.
  • Distributor and direct-buyer information mixed together, so neither knows how to order.

Search topics worth their own page

Overseas electronics and semiconductor buyers rarely search for a supplier by company type alone. They search for the product, the specification or the problem. These are the pages we usually plan first:

  • Part-number and series pages with parametric tables, so searches for a specific package, rating or interface land on a real product.
  • Cross-reference content that shows which of your parts replace common alternatives — only where the equivalence is verified by your engineers.
  • Compliance pages that list RoHS, REACH and other declarations per product family, with downloadable documents.
  • PCBA and EMS capability pages that state layer counts, component sizes, inspection methods and the files you need for a quote.
  • Application notes for the end market — automotive, industrial, consumer, medical — with the qualifications that apply to each.
  • Lifecycle and supply information: lead times, long-term availability and how you notify customers of changes.

Usually not. One site with separate sections for products and for manufacturing services keeps each buyer on the right path.
