Furniture in 2025: the industry and official figures

The figures come from the China National Furniture Association (CNFA), China’s national statistics and the Foshan bulletin.

  • The CNFA counts 7,467 furniture firms above designated size; their 2025 revenue was 612.51 billion yuan, down 10.7%, and profits 32.81 billion yuan, down 12.1%.[1]
  • Furniture output fell 4.0% in 2025: wooden furniture −7.9%, metal −4.7% and upholstered −4.3%.[1]
  • Furniture exports were US$67.81 billion, down 5.9%, which the CNFA links to trade frictions and US tariffs.[1]
  • National statistics put exports of furniture and parts at 455.9 billion yuan, down 5.6%.[2]
  • By province, Zhejiang made the most furniture, followed by Guangdong, Fujian, Jiangsu, Hebei, Shandong and Jiangxi.[1]
  • In Foshan, furniture value added fell 6.7%, while furniture exports were 18.93 billion yuan, up 1.1%.[3]

When the overall market shrinks, buyers do not stop buying; they become more selective. A supplier that shows compliance, material sources and finished projects clearly is easier to approve than one that only shows a catalogue.

What overseas buyers compare in furniture and interiors

Furniture buyers are importers, retailers, contract and hospitality buyers, and specifiers. They compare suppliers within groups like these:

Office and contract furniture

Seating, desks, systems and acoustic products, compared on durability tests, ergonomics, configurability and project capacity.

Hospitality and healthcare

Hotel, restaurant and care furniture with fire-performance, cleanability and project-management requirements.

Home furniture and upholstery

Collections for retailers and brands, compared on materials, finishes, packing and drop-ship ability.

Cabinets, millwork and fixtures

Kitchen cabinets, retail fixtures and architectural millwork made to drawings, where buyers check engineering and installation support.

Outdoor furniture

Materials, weather resistance and corrosion protection, with test evidence.

Hardware, components and materials

Fittings, mechanisms, panels and surface materials sold to furniture makers.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Rules vary by product and market, but these come up repeatedly with European and international buyers:

  • The EU Deforestation Regulation (EU) 2023/1115 names wood and derived products such as furniture; operators must be able to prove products do not come from recently deforested land. It applies to large and medium operators from 30 December 2026 and to micro and small operators from 30 June 2027.[4]
  • FSC chain of custody certification verifies that FSC-certified forest materials are tracked from forest to finished goods, and lets certified organisations use the FSC label and trademarks.[5]
  • The EU’s Digital Product Passport is being introduced by product group; the Commission’s timeline lists a furniture ecodesign delegated act for 2028.[6]
  • Google’s product structured data documentation explains how product pages can expose name, image, price and availability to search, which suits furniture collections.[7]

Only claim certificates you hold, for the products and materials they cover. Buyers can verify FSC certificates, and an inaccurate claim can end a relationship.

Questions overseas buyers ask furniture and interiors suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

What materials and finishes are used, and where does the timber come from?

Material specifications per product, timber species and the traceability or certification you can provide.

Which tests has this product passed?

Durability, stability or fire tests, with the standard, lab and report date.

Can you make to our design and drawings?

Your engineering and sampling process, and examples of custom projects.

How is it packed and how many fit a container?

Packing method, carton dimensions and container loading per product.

What is the MOQ and lead time?

Per product or collection, including sampling time.

Have you done projects like ours?

Project references by type: offices, hotels, schools, retail, with photos you are allowed to use.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for furniture and interiors manufacturers

Furniture is visual, but importers and contract buyers decide on data and reliability. This is the order we usually work in.

  1. Organise the site by buyer and use: contract, hospitality, home, outdoor, and by product type within each.
  2. Give each collection a page with dimensions, materials, finishes, packing data and downloadable specification sheets.
  3. Publish a compliance and materials page covering test reports, timber traceability and certificates, linked from products.
  4. Show project references with photos, locations and the products supplied, where clients allow.
  5. Write pages for the searches specifiers use, such as a product type plus a standard or a use case, not only “furniture factory”.
  6. Add an OEM and custom-project section that explains your design, sampling and quality-control process.
  7. Prepare for furniture fairs with show landing pages and follow-up content.
  8. Track which collections and project pages bring requests, and develop those first.

Questions

Questions from furniture and interiors manufacturers

Should we show prices on the website?

Most furniture exporters show MOQs and price bands or invite quotes. What matters is that buyers can tell quickly whether you fit their range and volume.

How do we prepare for the EU Deforestation Regulation online?

Make sure your timber sourcing and traceability information is accurate and ready to share, and present it clearly. The legal obligations sit with operators; your role is to supply the information they need.

Is FSC certification necessary?

Not always, but some buyers require it. If you hold it, show it properly with scope and certificate code; if not, explain what traceability you can provide.

Can we show furniture we make for other brands?

Only with permission, or anonymised. You can describe the type of work and capacity without naming clients.

Do contract buyers search online?

Specifiers and procurement teams research suppliers online before inviting them to tender, especially for hotels, offices and public buildings.

Can you help with 3D models or configurators?

We can plan and build product pages that host your models or configurators and make sure they load fast enough for overseas visitors.

Do you guarantee more orders?

No. We make your range easier to find and judge, and report actual inquiries.

Is Hurfi in Foshan?

No. Hurfi’s offices are in Xi’an and Bogura. We work with furniture makers in Guangdong, Zhejiang and elsewhere remotely.
