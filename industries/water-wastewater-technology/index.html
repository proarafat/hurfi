Water and wastewater: the figures behind demand

Demand for water technology is driven by regulation and by the condition of rivers, plants and pipes. The figures below come from China’s national statistics, Chinese city bulletins and the European Commission.

  • Of 3,641 national surface-water monitoring sections in China, 91.4% were rated good (Classes I–III) in 2025; 0.6% were rated worse than Class V.[1]
  • The EU counts 30,354 urban wastewater treatment plants in operation, and says 10 million Europeans still lack access to basic sanitation services.[2]
  • The revised EU Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive entered into force on 1 January 2025. It requires collection and treatment in all urban areas of more than 1,000 inhabitants and stricter treatment, including for new pollutants.[2]
  • The recast EU Drinking Water Directive (EU) 2020/2184 introduces a risk-based monitoring model aimed at emerging pollutants such as PFAS and microplastics.[3]
  • Taizhou in Zhejiang made 64.0521 million pumps in 2025 (−2.0%) and 232,000 tonnes of valves (−3.2%).[4]

Stricter rules abroad translate into more detailed questions for suppliers: what a membrane removes, what a pump’s wetted materials are, whether components are approved for drinking water. Suppliers who publish those answers save buyers a round of emails, and get shortlisted earlier.

What overseas buyers compare in water and wastewater

Water buyers usually arrive with a process problem or a specification. They compare suppliers within one of these groups:

Pumps and dosing

Duty points, efficiency curves, materials in contact with water or chemicals, seal options and spare-parts lead times.

Valves and fittings

Pressure ratings, connection standards, materials and approvals for drinking water or aggressive media.

Membranes and filtration

RO, UF and MBR membranes, cartridges and media: flux, rejection, cleaning regimes and replacement intervals.

Treatment systems

Packaged plants, sludge dewatering, aeration and disinfection: design basis, footprint, energy use and commissioning support.

Sensors, meters and controls

Water-quality sensors, smart meters, leak detection and control panels: accuracy, protocols and calibration.

Reuse and desalination

Components and integrated systems for reuse and seawater, where buyers ask for reference plants and operating data.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Which approvals apply depends on the product and the country. These are the references overseas water buyers most often bring up.

  • NSF/ANSI 61 sets minimum health-effects requirements for contaminants that products and materials can impart to drinking water. Its scope covers mechanical devices such as water meters, valves and filters, pipes and fittings, and process media such as filter media and ion-exchange resins. It does not set performance requirements.[5]
  • The recast EU Drinking Water Directive applies to water for domestic purposes and food production, from tap, tanker or bottle, and sets thresholds that the Commission says are often stricter than WHO guidelines.[3]
  • The revised EU Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive pushes for more nutrient removal, water reuse and resource recovery, and lower greenhouse-gas emissions from plants.[2]
  • Pumps, blowers and other machinery sold in the EU fall under the Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC; the new Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027.[6]
  • For the website itself, Google’s SEO Starter Guide recommends describing products in the words people actually search with, which in water usually means process terms and contaminants.[7]

A page should never say a product is “approved” in general. Say which approval, for which model and material, issued by whom, and link to the certificate or listing.

Questions overseas buyers ask water and wastewater suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

What does this unit remove, and to what level?

Publish design basis and test results for named contaminants, with the test conditions.

Which wetted materials are used, and are they approved for drinking water?

List materials per model and the approvals that apply to each.

Can you size a system for our flow and water quality?

An RFQ form that asks for flow, feed-water analysis and discharge limits gets you an answerable request.

What are the operating costs?

Energy use, chemical consumption and membrane or cartridge replacement intervals, with assumptions stated.

Where are your reference plants?

A references page by application and country, with what was supplied and since when.

Who commissions and services the equipment here?

Say whether you send engineers, use a local partner or support remotely, and how spare parts are shipped.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for water and wastewater manufacturers

Water equipment is specified by engineers and approved by utilities or plant owners, so the website has to serve both. This is the order we usually work in.

  1. Organise the site by application (municipal drinking water, municipal wastewater, industrial process water, reuse) and by product family, and cross-link the two.
  2. Give each product family a page with duty ranges, materials, performance tables and downloadable datasheets, with the key figures written on the page.
  3. Build an approvals page that maps each certificate or listing to the models and materials it covers.
  4. Write application pages for specific problems buyers search for, such as removing a named contaminant or treating a named industrial effluent.
  5. Publish case studies with feed and treated water parameters, where clients allow it, rather than photos alone.
  6. Use an RFQ form that collects flow, water analysis, discharge or product-water targets and site conditions.
  7. Track which application pages lead to RFQs in Search Console and analytics, and extend those first.

Questions

Questions from water and wastewater manufacturers

Should we organise the site by product or by application?

Both, cross-linked. Engineers often start from the application; purchasing teams often start from the product family.

Do we need NSF or EU approvals before we market abroad?

It depends on the product and market, and only you or your certification body can confirm what applies. The site should state clearly what you hold and for which models.

How much technical detail should be public?

Enough for an engineer to shortlist you: duty ranges, materials, performance data and dimensions. Detailed designs can stay behind an RFQ.

Can you write about water treatment processes accurately?

We write from your engineers’ material and recognised sources, and your team approves every technical claim before publication.

We supply OEM pumps to other brands. Can that be on the website?

Yes, as an OEM or ODM section that explains what you make for others and how, without naming customers who have not agreed.

Do you promise a number of RFQs per month?

No. We set targets with you and report actual searches, visits and inquiries from your own analytics.

Is Hurfi based in Taizhou or Wenzhou?

No. Hurfi’s offices are in Xi’an and Bogura. We work with manufacturers in any Chinese city remotely.
