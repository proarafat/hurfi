Precision manufacturing in 2025: the official figures

China’s statistics and city bulletins show growth in general equipment, moulds and additive manufacturing.

  • General-purpose equipment manufacturing value added grew 8.0% in 2025.[1]
  • 3D printing equipment output rose 52.5% to 5.211 million units, and industrial robot output 28.0%.[1]
  • Taizhou in Zhejiang made 105,500 sets of moulds, up 29.2%.[2]
  • Shaoxing made 1.707 billion sets of rolling bearings.[3]
  • Changzhou’s carbon fibre and composites output rose 46.6%.[4]

Many factories can make parts; fewer can show a buyer exactly what they can make, to what tolerance, and how they check it. Those that do get better RFQs.

What overseas buyers compare in precision manufacturing

Engineering buyers compare precision suppliers by process:

CNC machining

Axes, work envelopes, materials, tolerances and surface finishes.

Sheet metal and stamping

Press tonnage, progressive dies, bending and welding.

Casting and forging

Processes, alloys, sizes and heat treatment.

Injection moulding and tooling

Mould making, press range, materials and cycle times.

Additive manufacturing

Processes, materials, build sizes and post-processing.

Assemblies and finishing

Surface treatment, assembly and testing.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Tolerances and inspection are where precision buyers start:

  • ISO 2768-1 specifies general tolerances in four classes for linear and angular dimensions without individual tolerance indications, for parts made by metal removal or formed from sheet metal.[5]
  • ISO/IEC 17025 lets laboratories show that they operate competently and generate valid results, helping their reports be accepted across countries.[6]
  • Google lists PDF among the file types it can index, but key capability data should also be on HTML pages where buyers and search engines read it directly.[7]
  • Google’s product structured data documentation explains how to describe products for search, useful for standard components.[8]

State tolerances you achieve routinely, not best-ever results, and list inspection equipment and certifications with scope.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for precision manufacturing manufacturers

Precision buyers arrive with a drawing. The site should make it easy to send one and show why you can make it. Our usual order:

  1. Build capability pages by process, with machine lists, sizes, materials and tolerances.
  2. Publish a quality page with inspection equipment, certifications and sample reports.
  3. Show part galleries by industry and process, with permission.
  4. Use an RFQ form that accepts drawings securely and asks for material, quantity and finish.
  5. Write pages for searches such as a process plus a material plus an industry.
  6. Turn PDF catalogues into searchable web pages.
  7. Track which processes and industries bring good RFQs and expand those.

Questions

Questions from precision manufacturing manufacturers

Should we list our machines?

Yes. Machine lists and work envelopes help engineers decide quickly.

Can we show customer parts?

With permission, or with identifying features removed.

How do we handle drawing uploads safely?

Use a secure form, state your NDA policy and limit who can access files.

Is Google Ads worth it for machining?

For specific processes and markets, often yes. Generic terms tend to waste budget.

Should we publish prices?

Usually not; parts are quoted. Publish lead times and MOQs.

Will you guarantee RFQs?

No. We set targets with you and report real data.
