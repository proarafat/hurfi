Vehicles and components in 2025: the official figures

MIIT publishes the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers (CAAM) figures; the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) publishes output and customs exports.

  • China made 34.531 million vehicles and sold 34.40 million in 2025, up 10.4% and 9.4%; new-energy vehicles were 16.626 million made and 16.49 million sold, 47.9% of new-vehicle sales.[1]
  • Vehicle exports were 7.098 million units (+21.1%), including 2.615 million new-energy vehicles (+103.7%).[1]
  • The NBS puts output at 34.778 million vehicles (+9.8%) and customs exports of motor vehicles, including chassis, at 8.32 million units (+30.0%) worth 1,018.3 billion yuan (+22.0%).[2]
  • Anhui made 3.686 million vehicles, 1.794 million of them new-energy, both first among China’s provinces.[3]

The two series use different scopes, so quote one and name it. Either way, overseas buyers now know Chinese vehicles and search for the suppliers behind them by part, material and approval.

What overseas buyers compare in automotive components

Automotive buyers compare suppliers by system, process and channel:

Electrical and electronic parts

Wiring harnesses, connectors, ECUs, sensors, lighting and telematics: specifications, validation and software support.

Chassis, braking and suspension

Safety-relevant parts where approvals, test reports and traceability decide the shortlist.

Body, interior and glass

Stamping, injection moulding, seating, sealing and glazing: tooling, materials and finish.

Thermal, HVAC and filtration

Heat exchangers, compressors, filters and thermal management for combustion and electric vehicles.

EV and battery support parts

Battery enclosures, busbars, cooling plates and high-voltage components.

Aftermarket parts

OE cross-references, fitment, packaging and distributor terms, including commercial-vehicle and trailer parts.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

These references come up most with OEM, Tier 1 and European aftermarket buyers:

  • ISO/TS 16949 was created in 1999 by the International Automotive Task Force to harmonise assessment and certification systems across the automotive supply chain; IATF 16949:2016 replaced it and stays aligned with ISO 9001.[4]
  • Regulation (EU) 2018/858 governs the approval and market surveillance of motor vehicles and of systems, components and separate technical units intended for them.[5]
  • The EU End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation, which the Commission announced on 12 August 2026 was entering into force, sets recycled-plastic targets for new vehicles of 15% from 2032 and 25% from 2036, with targets for steel and aluminium to follow from 2033.[6]
  • The EU Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 covers electric-vehicle and other batteries, including those built into vehicles, with rules on safety, labelling, supply-chain due diligence and end of life.[7]
  • From 18 February 2027 a digital product passport becomes mandatory for certain batteries, including electric-vehicle batteries.[8]
  • Google’s product structured data guide lists the product properties a page can mark up, so put part numbers and specifications in readable text.[9]

Name a certificate, approval or E-mark only where you hold it, with scope and number. Approvals belong to specific products, not the whole factory.

Questions overseas buyers ask automotive components suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Are you IATF 16949 certified?

Show the certificate, the site it covers, the scope and the certification body.

Which OEM or Tier 1 programmes do you supply?

Name them only with permission; otherwise describe the vehicle segment and component.

Can you meet PPAP and APQP requirements?

Explain your launch process, documentation and sample submission levels.

Which parts carry E-mark or other approvals?

List approvals per part number, never as a blanket claim.

What is your capacity and tooling lead time?

Annual capacity per process, tooling ownership and typical lead times.

Can you cross-reference OE numbers?

For aftermarket parts, a searchable OE and fitment table saves buyers days.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for automotive components manufacturers

Engineering, quality and purchasing each look for different proof. Our usual order:

  1. Organise the site by vehicle system and process (harness, stamping, moulding, casting), with a separate route for aftermarket buyers.
  2. Give each part family a page with drawings or specification tables, materials, tolerances, test standards and part numbers in HTML, not only in PDFs.
  3. Publish a quality page: IATF 16949 and ISO certificates, PPAP levels, measurement and test equipment, and traceability.
  4. For EV parts, add pages that explain battery-regulation and passport questions factually, with links to the official sources.
  5. For aftermarket ranges, build OE cross-reference and fitment pages that search engines can read.
  6. Use an RFQ form that asks for drawing or OE number, annual volume, material, target market and start of production.
  7. Measure which systems and markets bring qualified RFQs in Search Console and analytics, and deepen those pages first.

Where in China automotive components is made

Official 2025 statistics point to vehicle and component activity in cities with Hurfi guides:

  • Chongqing: made 2.7877 million vehicles, 1.2961 million of them new-energy; automobile industry value added grew 12.6%.[10]
  • Wuhu: made 1.801 million cars (+10.2%) and 411,000 new-energy vehicles (+37.6%).[11]
  • Taizhou: made 226,900 cars (+72.6%) and 1.4979 million motorcycles.[12]
  • Guangzhou: new-energy vehicle output grew 21.6% while automobile manufacturing as a whole fell 1.6%; investment in auto parts rose 10.1%.[13]

Questions

Questions from automotive components manufacturers

Do overseas automotive buyers really source online?

Yes. Engineers search by part, material, process and approval, and check your website before an audit.

Should we publish part numbers and drawings?

Publish part numbers and drawings you own. Keep customer drawings and confidential programmes off the site.

Can we say we supply a well-known car brand?

Only with written permission. Otherwise describe the vehicle segment, the component and the volume.

Do we need IATF 16949 before we market abroad?

OEM and Tier 1 programmes usually ask for it; many aftermarket buyers accept ISO 9001 with strong test data. Say exactly what you hold.

How should aftermarket suppliers handle OE numbers?

Use OE numbers as cross-references, state that parts are replacements, and keep fitment data accurate and up to date.

Will you guarantee rankings or orders?

No. We improve how buyers find and evaluate you, and we report real search and enquiry data.
