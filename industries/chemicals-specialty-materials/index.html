Chemicals in 2025: the official and industry figures

China’s national statistics and the China Petroleum and Chemical Industry Federation (CPCIF) describe a large industry still growing in output.

  • Chemical raw materials and products value added grew 7.8% in 2025.[1]
  • Output included 110.816 million tonnes of sulphuric acid (+4.5%), 46.535 million tonnes of caustic soda (+5.0%), 41.508 million tonnes of ethylene (+6.4%) and 64.808 million tonnes of fertiliser (+7.1%).[1]
  • The CPCIF reports petroleum and chemical industry revenue of 15.67 trillion yuan in 2025 and profits of 702.09 billion yuan; value added grew 6.9%, one point faster than industry as a whole.[2]
  • Apparent consumption of ethylene grew 7.7%; the CPCIF says export volumes grew quickly even as total trade value fell.[2]
  • City bulletins show fast growth in chemical raw materials and products in Yantai (+46.2%), Qingdao (+15.9%) and Weifang (+11.4%).[3][4][5]

Rising volumes and falling trade values mean more competition on price. Producers who document grades, purity, consistency and regulatory status clearly give buyers reasons beyond price to qualify them.

What overseas buyers compare in chemicals and specialty materials

Chemical buyers compare suppliers grade by grade within product families:

Specialty and fine chemicals

Purity, impurity profiles, batch consistency and analytical methods.

Polymers and resins

Grades, melt flow or viscosity, additives and processing guidance.

Coatings, inks and adhesives

Performance data, application methods, VOC content and approvals.

Intermediates and agrochemical inputs

Specifications, registrations and supply security.

Advanced and electronic materials

High-purity materials, battery and semiconductor inputs: purity, traceability and qualification support.

Water-treatment and process chemicals

Dosing, performance in application and safety handling.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Regulatory status is often the first filter. These references matter for European and international buyers:

  • REACH (EC 1907/2006) is the EU’s main law on chemicals; companies must register substances, ECHA and Member States evaluate the information, and substances of very high concern can be authorised or restricted.[6]
  • OEKO-TEX says its chemical requirements cover REACH Annexes XVII and XIV, CPSIA and the ECHA candidate list, which matters for suppliers to textile mills.[7]
  • ISO/IEC 17025 lets testing and calibration laboratories show that they operate competently and generate valid results, and helps their reports be accepted from one country to another, which is why buyers ask whether certificates of analysis come from an accredited lab.[8]
  • Google’s guidance on helpful, people-first content favours pages that show first-hand expertise, which suits detailed technical-data and application pages.[9]

Never state or imply REACH registration, food-contact status or other approvals you do not hold. Name each registration or approval, its scope and the grade it applies to.

Questions overseas buyers ask chemicals and specialty materials suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Can you send the TDS, SDS and a certificate of analysis?

Make technical and safety data sheets available per grade, and explain how COAs are issued.

What is your REACH or local registration status?

State it per substance and market.

How consistent is quality from batch to batch?

Explain your QC methods, equipment and lab accreditation.

What packaging and minimum quantities do you offer?

Drums, IBCs, bags or bulk, with MOQs.

Can you develop a custom grade?

Describe R&D capability and typical development time.

How do you ship dangerous goods?

Classification, packaging and the shipping documents you provide.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for chemicals and specialty materials manufacturers

Chemical buyers search by CAS number, grade name and application, and expect documents on request. Our usual order:

  1. Create a page per product or grade family with CAS numbers, specifications, typical properties and applications in text.
  2. Offer TDS and SDS downloads, gated or open, and make clear how to request COAs.
  3. Publish a regulatory page that states registrations and approvals per substance and market.
  4. Write application pages for the problems buyers solve with your materials.
  5. Show QC and lab capability, including accreditation where held.
  6. Use an RFQ form that asks for grade, quantity, packaging, destination and documents needed.
  7. Track which grades and applications bring inquiries, and expand those first.

Questions

Questions from chemicals and specialty materials manufacturers

Should we publish SDS and TDS openly?

Many producers publish TDS openly and supply SDS on request. Either way, buyers should see that the documents exist.

Can we target searches by CAS number?

Yes. CAS numbers and grade names are among the most specific searches serious buyers use.

How do we present REACH status?

Exactly: substance, registration status and the role you play (manufacturer, importer via representative). Do not claim registration you lack.

Can you write chemical content accurately?

Yes, from your technical documents; your chemists approve every claim before publication.

Is Google Ads useful for chemicals?

For specific grades and applications in selected markets, often yes. Broad terms tend to waste budget.

Will you guarantee rankings?

No. We target specific searches and report results from your own Search Console.

Is Hurfi located in Shandong?

No. Hurfi’s offices are in Xi’an and Bogura. We work with producers in any province remotely.
