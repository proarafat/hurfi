Export marketing for Chinese battery and energy storage manufacturers

Battery and storage buyers abroad check chemistry, test data, certification and shipping documents before they trust a new supplier. Hurfi helps Chinese manufacturers publish that information clearly and reach buyers through search and LinkedIn.

What overseas buyers check before they contact a battery and energy storage supplier

  • Cell chemistry (for example LFP or NMC), capacity, voltage and the conditions behind any cycle-life figure.
  • The battery management system: protections, communication interfaces and compatibility with common inverters.
  • Transport and safety documents: UN38.3 test summaries, safety data sheets, and certificates such as IEC 62619 or UL 9540 only where you hold them.
  • How packs and containers ship as dangerous goods, and what documentation travels with them.
  • Warranty terms in years or cycles, and how warranty claims are handled abroad.

What the website and search work should include

  • Product pages by application — residential, commercial and industrial, telecom, UPS — with specification tables in HTML.
  • A document centre with test summaries and certificates, each with its scope and date.
  • Clear pages for OEM and private-label work, separate from your own-brand products.
  • Search work on specification and compatibility questions, such as inverter pairing or rack formats.
  • LinkedIn content showing production, testing and real project deliveries you are allowed to share.

Gaps we often see

  • Cycle-life and efficiency numbers with no test conditions, which experienced buyers discount immediately.
  • Certificates shown as logos only, without the certificate holder, model coverage or validity.
  • No information on shipping and dangerous-goods handling, which is one of the first practical questions.

Search topics worth their own page

Overseas battery and energy storage buyers rarely search for a supplier by company type alone. They search for the product, the specification or the problem. These are the pages we usually plan first:

  • Chemistry and format pages — LFP, NMC, sodium-ion; prismatic, cylindrical, pouch — with the specifications buyers filter by.
  • Certification pages that list UN 38.3, IEC 62619, UL 9540 or other marks per product, with certificate numbers where you hold them.
  • Application pages for residential storage, commercial and industrial storage, telecom backup and mobility, each with typical system sizes.
  • BMS and integration content: communication protocols, inverter compatibility and how monitoring works.
  • Shipping and logistics information for dangerous goods, which overseas buyers ask about before they order samples.
  • Warranty and cycle-life pages that state the test conditions behind the numbers.
