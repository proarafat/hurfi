Export marketing for Chinese EV and e-mobility manufacturers

Distributors, installers and fleet operators abroad compare EV equipment suppliers on standards, software and after-sales support. Hurfi helps Chinese manufacturers present those answers clearly in English and get found by the right buyers.

Book a Consultation →

What overseas buyers check before they contact a EV and e-mobility supplier

  • Power ratings, connector standards (such as CCS, CHAdeMO, Type 2, GB/T or NACS) and which markets each model is built for.
  • Charge-point software: the OCPP version supported, back-end compatibility and how firmware updates are delivered.
  • Certifications held for each target market, with the certificate holder and the model numbers covered.
  • Installation manuals, wiring diagrams and the documents an electrician or installer needs.
  • Warranty terms, spare parts and who handles service in the buyer’s country.

What the website and search work should include

  • Model pages organised by use case — home, workplace, fleet depot, public fast charging — with a comparison table.
  • A documentation library that installers can use without emailing sales.
  • A partner page for distributors and installers that explains margins, training and stock support in plain terms.
  • Search work focused on model, standard and compatibility questions in each export market.
  • LinkedIn and video content from real installations, with permission from the site owner.

Gaps we often see

  • One global product page with no indication of which plug standard or certification applies where.
  • Software and back-end questions left unanswered, although they decide many fleet purchases.
  • No clear statement of after-sales support outside China.

Search topics worth their own page

Overseas EV and e-mobility buyers rarely search for a supplier by company type alone. They search for the product, the specification or the problem. These are the pages we usually plan first:

  • Component pages by vehicle system — motors, controllers, chargers, harnesses, thermal parts — with the voltages and power ranges you build.
  • Market pages that state which standards and type approvals apply in Europe, North America or other regions, and which you already meet.
  • Charging-equipment pages with connector types, power levels and communication protocols spelled out.
  • Light electric vehicle content for e-bike, scooter and motorcycle makers, which is a different buyer from passenger-car OEMs.
  • PPAP, sample and validation process pages for automotive buyers who need to know how a part gets approved.
  • Plain-English explanations of your testing: durability, environmental and safety tests, and who carries them out.

Questions factories ask

Our buyers are mostly distributors. What should the website do for them?

Give them the documents, specifications and partner terms they need to choose you and to sell your product to their own customers.

Can the site serve several markets with different standards?

Yes. Market-specific pages or filters can show which models and certificates apply in each region.

Will you promise a number of inquiries?

No. We can promise clear work and honest reporting, not a number of inquiries.
