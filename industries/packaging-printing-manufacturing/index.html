Packaging and printing in 2025: the official figures

Chinese statistics show the scale of plastics and parcel volumes; the European Commission shows how the rules for packaging are changing.

  • China’s plastic product exports were 748.7 billion yuan in 2025, down 0.8%, and express delivery reached 199 billion items.[1]
  • The EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (EU) 2025/40 has been in force since 11 February 2025 and applies from 12 August 2026.[2]
  • The Commission says packaging waste reached a record 186.5 kg per person in the EU in 2022; 40% of plastics used in the EU are in packaging, and half of marine litter comes from packaging.[2]
  • The regulation aims to make all packaging on the EU market recyclable in an economically viable way by 2030 and to increase the safe use of recycled plastics.[2]
  • In Quanzhou, paper and printing value added grew 13.4%, one of three of the city’s nine 100-billion-yuan industries with double-digit growth.[3]

For suppliers, the rules turn packaging specifications into compliance questions. Buyers will ask about materials, recyclability and documentation; suppliers who answer on their website get shortlisted first.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

These references come up most with European buyers:

  • The EU packaging regulation sets mandatory rules for eco-design, recycled content, waste prevention, reuse formats and collection and recycling, including binding reuse targets and deposit return systems.[2]
  • All food contact materials in the EU must comply with Regulation (EC) 1935/2004 and be made under good manufacturing practice (Regulation (EC) 2023/2006); specific rules apply to plastics.[4]
  • FSC chain of custody certification tracks FSC-certified material from forest to finished product and allows certified companies to use the FSC label.[5]
  • Google’s SEO Starter Guide recommends using the words people search with, such as material and format names, not only internal product codes.[6]

Claim recyclability, recycled content or certifications only where you can document them, per material and product.

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Explain material structure and what you can document.

Which materials, what percentage and how it is certified.

Is it approved for food contact?

Declarations of compliance per material and use.

What are your MOQs and print options?

MOQs, print methods, colours and finishes.

How fast can you sample?

Sampling and production lead times.

Do you hold FSC or other certificates?

Certificates with scope and codes.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for packaging and printing manufacturers

Packaging buyers are brand, procurement and sustainability teams. Each needs different evidence. Our usual order:

  1. Organise the site by material and format, and by end market (food, cosmetics, e-commerce, industrial).
  2. Give each product family a page with structures, sizes, print options and MOQs.
  3. Publish a compliance and sustainability page with food-contact, recyclability and certification information.
  4. Show case studies of packaging redesigns with before and after details, where permitted.
  5. Write pages on the regulation-driven questions buyers search for, without giving legal advice.
  6. Use an RFQ form that asks for format, size, material, print and quantity.
  7. Track which materials and formats bring requests and expand those.
