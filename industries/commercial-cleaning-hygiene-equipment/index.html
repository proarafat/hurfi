Cleaning equipment and service robots in 2025: the figures

China’s statistics show fast growth in service robots, which include many cleaning robots, while EU rules shape what buyers ask.

  • China made 18.581 million service robot sets in 2025, up 16.1%.[1]
  • Beijing’s service robot output rose 47.6%, and Tianjin’s 6.4%.[2][3]
  • IEC 60335-2-72:2021 covers the safety of powered ride-on and walk-behind floor treatment machines for commercial use, for sweeping, scrubbing, wet or dry pick-up, polishing and shampooing floors.[4]
  • The standard covers machines used in hotels, schools, hospitals, factories, shops and offices, powered by combustion engines, mains motors (up to 250 V single-phase or 480 V other) or batteries.[4]
  • In the EU, 14.4 million tonnes of electrical and electronic equipment are placed on the market, and 5 million tonnes of e-waste were collected in 2022.[5]

Automation is changing the sector, but buyers still want proven safety, productivity and service. Those are what a supplier’s website must show.

Availability and compatibility.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Safety standards and EU rules drive buyer questions:

  • IEC 60335-2-72 does not apply to household floor machines, road sweepers or machines for explosive environments, so manufacturers should state exactly which standards each model is tested to.[4]
  • Powered cleaning machines sold in the EU fall under the Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC; the Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027.[6]
  • Mains-powered equipment between 50 and 1000 V AC falls under the Low Voltage Directive, and the EMC Directive covers electromagnetic compatibility.[7][8]
  • The recast WEEE Directive entered into force on 13 August 2012 and governs the collection and recycling of electrical equipment at end of life.[5]

Publish safety standards, test reports and declarations per model. Buyers and dealers will ask for them before stocking.

Questions overseas buyers ask commercial cleaning and hygiene equipment suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Which safety standards has this model been tested to?

Standards, test lab and report per model.

How many square metres per hour does it clean?

Productivity figures with conditions.

How long does the battery last?

Run time, charging time and battery type.

What support do dealers get?

Training, parts, warranty and marketing.

How does the robot navigate and stay safe?

Sensors, mapping and safety functions.

How fast are spare parts delivered?

Stock and delivery times.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for commercial cleaning and hygiene equipment manufacturers

Cleaning equipment sells through dealers and to contractors. The site must serve both. Our usual order:

  1. Organise by machine type and by application (retail, healthcare, industrial, hospitality).
  2. Give each model a page with specifications, productivity, battery data, videos and downloads.
  3. Publish a compliance page with standards, test reports and declarations per model.
  4. Build a dealer page with territories, support and application process.
  5. Add a parts and service section.
  6. Write application pages for the searches buyers use.
  7. Track which models and markets bring dealer and buyer inquiries.
