Agriculture and agri-equipment in 2025: the official figures

China’s statistics show a large farm sector with steady output, and city bulletins show strong growth in agricultural processing in machinery-making areas.

  • China’s grain output was 714.88 million tonnes in 2025, up 1.2% or 8.38 million tonnes.[1]
  • Fertiliser output was 64.808 million tonnes (+7.1%), and agricultural and food processing value added grew 5.6%. Special-purpose equipment manufacturing grew 4.3%.[1]
  • The CPCIF reports that apparent fertiliser consumption grew 1.4%.[2]
  • In Weifang, general-purpose equipment grew 13.3% and agricultural and food processing 12.1%.[3]
  • The China-Africa Economic and Trade Expo, held in Changsha, lists modern agricultural machinery among its themes; the first three expos drew 53 African countries.[4]

Export markets for agricultural equipment are often far from China, with dealers who need parts, training and documentation. Manufacturers who show those things online are easier to sign with.

What overseas buyers compare in agritech and agricultural equipment

Agricultural buyers compare suppliers within equipment groups and for their farming conditions:

Tractors and power units

Power range, engine emissions stage, transmission and dealer support.

Implements and harvesting

Working width, soil conditions, durability and spare parts.

Irrigation and water management

Drip, sprinkler and pivot systems, pumps and controllers.

Greenhouses and controlled environments

Structures, climate control and lighting, with design support.

Drones and precision agriculture

Spraying, mapping, sensors and software, plus local regulations.

Post-harvest and livestock equipment

Drying, storage, feed and processing equipment.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Approval rules vary widely by country. These references matter for Europe and markets that follow EU rules:

  • Regulation (EU) No 167/2013 governs the approval and market surveillance of agricultural and forestry vehicles; it has been amended several times, including in 2019 and 2024.[5]
  • A 2025 Commission delegated regulation amended vehicle braking and functional safety requirements for agricultural and forestry vehicles.[5]
  • Implements and other agricultural machinery fall under the EU Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC; the Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027.[6]
  • Google’s guidance on multi-regional and multilingual sites explains how to serve separate language and country versions, important when dealers are in many countries.[7]

State which approvals you hold for which models and markets. Dealers will ask before they stock your machines.

Questions overseas buyers ask agritech and agricultural equipment suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

How quickly can we get spare parts?

Parts stock, ordering process and shipping times.

What dealer support do you offer?

Training, marketing material, warranty handling and margins.

Is it approved for our market?

Approvals and homologation status per model and country.

Will it work in our conditions?

Soil, crop, climate and terrain where the machines are proven.

Where are your machines already working?

References by country and crop.

What are the service intervals and costs?

Maintenance schedules and parts prices.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for agritech and agricultural equipment manufacturers

Agricultural equipment sells through dealers, so the site must recruit and support them as well as reach farmers. Our usual order:

  1. Organise the site by machine type and by crop or application.
  2. Give each model a page with specifications, attachments, photos and videos in the field.
  3. Build a dealer page covering territories, support, training and how to apply.
  4. Publish a parts and service section with manuals, parts lists and contact details.
  5. Add market pages in local languages for the countries you target.
  6. Show references by country and crop with farmer or dealer stories where permitted.
  7. Track which models and markets bring dealer and buyer inquiries.

Questions

Questions from agritech and agricultural equipment manufacturers

Should we target farmers or dealers?

Usually dealers first, with content that also helps farmers. Dealers need proof that farmers will want the machines.

Which languages do we need?

The languages of your target markets, translated properly, with local contact details.

Can we show machines working abroad?

Yes, with permission. Field videos are very persuasive for this sector.

How do we handle homologation questions online?

State approvals per model and market, and say what is in progress.

Is Google Ads useful for agricultural machinery?

In selected markets and seasons, for specific machine types, often yes.

Will you guarantee dealers?

No. We make you easier to find and evaluate, and report actual inquiries.

No. Hurfi’s offices are in Xi’an and Bogura; we work with manufacturers anywhere in China remotely.
