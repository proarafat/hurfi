Export marketing for Chinese automation and robotics manufacturers

Integrators and machine builders abroad shortlist automation suppliers on specifications and support. Hurfi helps Chinese manufacturers in this sector publish those details in English, get found in search, and turn visits into qualified inquiries.

Book a Consultation →

What overseas buyers check before they contact a automation and robotics supplier

  • Payload, reach, repeatability and cycle-time figures, with the test conditions behind them.
  • Which controllers, fieldbuses and protocols the product supports — for example EtherCAT, PROFINET, EtherNet/IP or Modbus — and whether drivers or libraries are available.
  • Safety information: the standards the product is designed and tested to (such as ISO 10218 for industrial robots or ISO/TS 15066 for collaborative applications) and the certificates you actually hold.
  • CAD models, manuals and integration guides that an engineer can download before speaking to sales.
  • Who supports the installation abroad: your own engineers, a distributor, or remote commissioning.

What the website and search work should include

  • A product page per robot or drive family, with a specification table in HTML rather than only in a PDF.
  • Application pages written for the buyer’s task — palletizing, machine tending, welding, inspection — that link to the products used.
  • An integrator or distributor page that says what partners get: pricing structure, training, demo units, technical support hours.
  • Search work aimed at specification-stage queries (model comparisons, protocol compatibility, payload classes) rather than broad “robot manufacturer” terms.
  • LinkedIn posts that show real cells running, with the application named, so engineers can judge the product before they ask.

Gaps we often see

  • Specifications only inside a downloadable catalog, so search engines and buyers cannot read them on the page.
  • Safety and certification claims with no certificate number, issuing body or scope.
  • One contact form for every request, so a quote, a support ticket and a partnership inquiry all land in the same inbox.

Search topics worth their own page

Overseas automation and robotics buyers rarely search for a supplier by company type alone. They search for the product, the specification or the problem. These are the pages we usually plan first:

  • Model and payload-class pages, such as six-axis robots by payload range, so an integrator searching for a specific class lands on the right product.
  • Protocol and controller compatibility pages: which PLCs, fieldbuses and software your robots or drives work with, written as plain answers.
  • Application pages for the cell the buyer is building — palletizing, welding, machine tending, inspection — with cycle-time figures and the products used.
  • Comparison content that explains when a SCARA, delta, six-axis or collaborative robot fits, in neutral language engineers trust.
  • Service and spare-parts pages that say how long parts are supported and how remote diagnosis works.
  • Downloadable CAD and manual libraries that are indexed by product, so engineers can find files by model number.
