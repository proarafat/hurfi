HVAC in 2025: the official figures

China’s national statistics and city bulletins show the scale of production; the European Commission shows why requirements abroad keep tightening.

  • China made 266.975 million room air conditioners in 2025, up 0.7%; general-purpose equipment manufacturing value added grew 8.0% and electrical machinery 9.2%.[1]
  • Wuhu in Anhui made 16.996 million air conditioners and 75.263 million compressors.[2]
  • The EU cut the supply of HFCs, the main fluorinated gases, by 45% between 2015 and 2024; HFC imports inside refrigeration, air-conditioning and heat-pump equipment were 10% lower in 2024 than in 2023.[3]
  • The Commission says 85% of EU buildings were built before 2000 and 75% have poor energy performance; around 40% of energy consumed in the EU is used in buildings.[4]
  • The annual energy renovation rate of EU buildings is about 1%, which the revised directive aims to raise.[4]

For exporters this means demand for efficient, low-GWP equipment and controls, and buyers who ask detailed questions about refrigerants, efficiency and integration before they ask about price.

What overseas buyers compare in HVAC and building systems

HVAC buyers compare suppliers within system groups, usually against a project specification:

Chillers and heat pumps

Capacity, seasonal efficiency, refrigerant and GWP, operating range and noise.

Air handling and ventilation

Airflow, pressure, heat recovery efficiency, filtration classes and casing performance.

Commercial and industrial refrigeration

Condensing units, cold rooms and display cases: refrigerant, temperature range and energy use.

Fans, coils and components

Fan curves, motor efficiency, coil materials and certified performance data.

Controls and building automation

Protocols supported, integration with building management systems and remote monitoring.

Cleanroom and process cooling

Precision temperature and humidity control for factories, labs and data rooms.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Rules depend on the product and the market. These are the references HVAC buyers in Europe raise most often:

  • EU F-gas rules restrict fluorinated refrigerants; the Commission promotes climate-friendly alternatives, and buyers increasingly ask which refrigerant a unit uses and why.[3]
  • The revised Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EU/2024/1275) must be transposed by 29 May 2026 and introduces requirements for building automation and control in non-residential buildings.[4]
  • Pumps, fans, compressors and other machinery fall under the EU Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC; the Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027.[5]
  • Electrical equipment between 50 and 1000 V AC falls under the Low Voltage Directive, which covers most HVAC units and controls.[6]

Show certified performance data and declarations per model, with the standard, the test body and the date. A single “CE certified” logo answers none of a consultant’s questions.

Questions overseas buyers ask HVAC and building systems suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

What refrigerant does it use, and what is its GWP?

State refrigerant and charge per model, and your plans for low-GWP alternatives.

What is the seasonal efficiency at our conditions?

Publish performance tables at standard rating points and offer selection software or sizing help.

Is the performance data independently certified?

Say which certification scheme or lab tested which models.

Will it integrate with our building management system?

Protocols, interfaces and examples of integrations.

What are lead times and spare-parts arrangements?

Production and shipping times, local stock and how spares are supplied.

Who commissions and services it here?

Local partners, training for installers and remote diagnostics.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for HVAC and building systems manufacturers

HVAC is specified by consultants, installed by contractors and bought by distributors or owners. The site has to serve all three, in this order of work:

  1. Structure the site by system type and by application (commercial buildings, industrial process, cold chain, residential).
  2. Give each product range a page with capacity tables, efficiency figures, refrigerant data, dimensions and downloadable submittal documents.
  3. Publish a compliance and certification page that maps each declaration or certificate to models.
  4. Offer selection help: sizing tools, BIM or CAD files, or a form that collects load, climate and application.
  5. Write application pages for the searches engineers use, such as heat pumps for a building type or cooling for a process.
  6. Show projects with photos, capacity and location where clients allow it.
  7. Track which ranges and applications produce inquiries in Search Console and analytics, and expand those first.

Questions

Questions from HVAC and building systems manufacturers

Should we publish full performance tables?

Yes for standard rating points. Consultants shortlist on data; detailed selections can follow by request.

How do we present refrigerant changes?

Accurately: which models use which refrigerant now, and what is planned. Do not announce products that are not available.

Do we need separate sites for distributors and consultants?

No. One site with clear sections and downloads for each audience usually works.

Can you write technical HVAC content?

Yes, from your engineers’ material and data sheets; your team approves every technical statement.

Which markets should we start with?

Those where you already have distributors or approvals. Each market page should answer local rules and climate questions.

Will you guarantee rankings or orders?

No. We improve how you are found and evaluated and report real search and inquiry data.

Does Hurfi have an office near our factory?
