Aerospace and aviation in 2025: the official figures

National and city statistics show aviation activity and aerospace manufacturing growing in several Chinese centres.

  • Civil aviation freight in China reached 10.172 million tonnes in 2025, up 13.3%.[1]
  • Suzhou’s aerospace equipment output grew 14.0%, and Nanjing’s aerospace manufacturing output 17.7%.[2][3]
  • Tianjin’s aerospace industrial chain grew 9.7%.[4]
  • Liaoning province’s rail, ship, aerospace and other transport equipment manufacturing grew 41.8%.[5]
  • Chengdu’s plan report says aerospace exports grew fast and lists an aero-engine industrial park among the projects under construction.[6]

Growth at home builds capability, but export work depends on passing a customer’s supplier qualification. The website will not do that for you; it can make sure the right people find you and see the evidence they need to start.

What overseas buyers compare in aerospace and aviation

Aerospace buyers compare suppliers by process and part family, within tight qualification rules:

Precision machining

Materials (titanium, aluminium, nickel alloys), multi-axis capacity, inspection and first-article reports.

Composites

Layup, curing, NDT and material qualifications.

Cabin interiors and seating parts

Flammability testing, weight and finish.

Electrical harnesses and components

Wiring standards, testing and traceability.

Tooling and ground support equipment

Design, load testing and certification for use around aircraft.

Special processes

Heat treatment, surface treatment and NDT, often needing specific accreditation.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Quality and approval systems are the first filter for aerospace buyers:

  • The IAQG 9100 series standardises quality management requirements for aviation, space and defence organisations, with 9110 for maintenance organisations and 9120 for distributors.[7]
  • EASA is responsible for certification and oversight of production organisations whose principal place of business is outside EU Member States, under Regulation (EU) 2018/1139 and Commission Regulation (EU) 748/2012.[8]
  • ISO/IEC 17025 helps laboratory test reports be accepted from one country to another, relevant to material and NDT testing.[9]
  • Google’s guidance on helpful content asks whether pages show first-hand expertise, which in aerospace means real process capability rather than generic claims.[10]

List only the certifications and approvals you hold, with scope, certificate numbers and validity. Never imply customer approvals you do not have; aerospace buyers check.

Questions overseas buyers ask aerospace and aviation suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Are you certified to 9100?

State the standard, certification body, scope and expiry.

Which materials and processes can you handle?

List materials, machines, special processes and inspection equipment.

Can you supply first-article inspection and full traceability?

Explain your documentation and traceability system.

What export-control and data-security measures do you have?

Describe how drawings and data are handled.

Which programmes or customers have you supplied?

Only where permitted; otherwise describe part types and volumes.

How do you manage lead times and changes?

Planning, capacity and communication arrangements.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for aerospace and aviation manufacturers

Aerospace marketing is about credibility and precision. Overclaiming is the fastest way to lose a buyer. Our usual order:

  1. Build capability pages by process and material, with machine lists, tolerances and inspection equipment.
  2. Publish a quality page with certifications, scope and validity, and an outline of your quality system.
  3. Show part families with photos you are allowed to publish, without customer drawings.
  4. Explain documentation: first-article reports, certificates of conformity and traceability.
  5. Write for supplier-quality and procurement searches, such as a process plus a material plus a standard.
  6. Use an RFQ form that accepts drawings securely and asks for material, quantity and required certifications.
  7. Track which capability pages bring qualified inquiries and expand those.

Questions

Questions from aerospace and aviation manufacturers

Can a website help us get qualified by aerospace customers?

It cannot replace qualification audits. It helps the right buyers find you and see enough evidence to start the process.

Should we list our machines?

Yes. Machine lists, work envelopes and inspection equipment are among the most useful details for aerospace buyers.

How do we handle confidential parts?

Show part types and processes, not customer drawings. Use secure upload for RFQs.

Do aerospace buyers use search engines?

Supplier-development and procurement engineers do, especially for special processes and capacity.

Can we mention programmes we supply?

Only with written permission. Many customers do not allow it.

Will you guarantee contracts?

No. We improve visibility and credibility and report real inquiry data.

Is Hurfi located near aerospace clusters?

Hurfi’s offices are in Xi’an and Bogura. We work with suppliers anywhere in China remotely.
