Mining and minerals in 2025: the official and industry figures

National statistics show steady mining output but falling profits, and the construction machinery association shows strong export growth.

  • China produced 4.85 billion tonnes of raw coal in 2025 (+1.4%) and 81.75 million tonnes of ten non-ferrous metals (+3.9%).[1]
  • Mining value added grew 5.6%, but mining profits fell 26.2% to 834.5 billion yuan.[1]
  • The CCMA reports 235,257 excavators sold in 2025 (+17%), 116,739 of them exported (+16.1%), and 61,737 loaders exported (+14.6%).[2]
  • Hunan has found 154 kinds of minerals, and its concrete machinery output rose 39.6%.[3]
  • Yantai’s non-ferrous metal smelting and rolling grew 15.7%.[4]

Lower domestic mining profits push equipment makers to export. Buyers in mining countries need proof of performance in similar ore and conditions, and reliable parts supply.

What overseas buyers compare in mining and mineral processing

Mining buyers compare suppliers by process stage:

Crushing and screening

Capacity, feed size, wear parts and mobility.

Grinding and classification

Mill sizes, power, liners and energy use.

Flotation and separation

Recovery, reagent use and cell sizes.

Dewatering and tailings

Thickeners, filters and dry stacking.

Underground and surface machinery

Loaders, drills and haulage, with safety features.

Wear parts and spares

Materials, life and availability.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Safety rules matter most for underground and processing equipment:

  • The ATEX Directive 2014/34/EU covers equipment and protective systems for potentially explosive atmospheres, and the Commission names mines among the places where such atmospheres occur.[5]
  • Mining and processing machinery sold in the EU falls under the Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC; the Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027.[6]
  • Google’s guidance on multi-regional and multilingual sites helps when you target several mining countries in different languages.[7]

Publish safety and compliance information per model and market, and be clear about what applies where.

Questions overseas buyers ask mining and mineral processing suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

Has this equipment worked on ore like ours?

References by mineral, capacity and country.

What throughput and wear life can we expect?

Performance data with assumptions.

How fast can you supply wear parts?

Stock, lead times and local warehouses.

Who commissions and services it on site?

Field service, training and remote support.

Can you supply a full plant?

EPC capability, design and integration.

What are the energy requirements?

Power and consumption per unit.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for mining and mineral processing manufacturers

Mining equipment sales are long and technical, with site visits and trials. The website builds credibility before that. Our usual order:

  1. Organise by process stage and by mineral.
  2. Give each machine a page with capacities, sizes, power and wear-part information.
  3. Publish case studies by mineral and country, with photos and data where allowed.
  4. Build a parts and service section with stock locations and response times.
  5. Add market pages for the mining countries you target, in their languages.
  6. Use an RFQ form that asks for ore type, capacity, feed size and site conditions.
  7. Track which machines and markets bring inquiries.

Those where your equipment suits the minerals and conditions, and where you can provide service.

How do we show performance without client names?

Describe mineral, capacity, country and results; name clients only with permission.
