OEM or ODM: say which one you are

Brand owners search differently depending on what they need. An OEM buyer already has a design and wants a factory to build it to their drawings. An ODM buyer wants a factory that brings its own designs, platforms or engineering to the project. Many factories do both, but the website should say clearly which services you offer, for which product categories, and where the line sits between your intellectual property and the client’s.

What brand owners check before they send a brief

  • Product categories and processes you run in-house, and which steps you subcontract.
  • Minimum order quantities, sampling cost and lead time for a first run, and how those change at volume.
  • How you handle confidentiality: NDAs, restricted areas, file handling, and whether you will sign the client’s own agreement.
  • Who owns tooling, moulds and design files, and what happens to them if the relationship ends.
  • Quality system and audit history: the certificates you hold, and whether you accept third-party and client audits.
  • Packaging, labelling and private-label options, including compliance marks for the target market.

Proof without naming clients

Most OEM contracts stop a factory from naming the brands it builds for. That does not leave you with nothing to show. The website can still carry:

  • Process pages with real photos of the lines, equipment lists and capacity figures you are willing to publish.
  • Anonymised project write-ups: the product type, the market, what the client asked for and what you changed — with the client’s permission.
  • Audit and certification documents with scope and expiry dates.
  • A clear description of the development process, from brief and quote to sample, pilot run and mass production.

We never invent clients or results. If a project cannot be described, it stays off the site.

Questions

Questions factories ask

Do we need to name our clients on the website?

No. Most OEM agreements forbid it. We build proof around processes, certificates, capacity and anonymised projects instead.

Should OEM and ODM be on separate pages?

If you offer both, yes: the buyer, the questions and the proof are different. One page each keeps the message clear.

Do you guarantee project inquiries?

No. We make sure brand owners can find and understand your capability, and we report what search and inquiries show.

Can you write in our technical language?

We write in plain English for buyers and ask your engineers to review every technical claim before it is published.
