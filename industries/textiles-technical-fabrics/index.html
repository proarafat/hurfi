Textiles in 2025: China’s output and exports

China remains the largest producer of yarn, cloth and chemical fibre. The national and city figures below are from official statistical bulletins.

  • China produced 22.158 million tonnes of yarn (−0.1%), 30.67 billion metres of cloth (+0.2%) and 87.011 million tonnes of chemical fibre (+4.9%) in 2025.[1]
  • Exports of textile yarn, fabrics and made-up articles were 1,020.5 billion yuan, up 1.0%, while garment exports fell 4.4%.[1]
  • Shaoxing made 26.349 billion metres of printed and dyed cloth and 3.767 million tonnes of chemical fibre.[2]
  • Nantong made 6.501 million tonnes of chemical fibre, and Fuzhou 3.5754 million tonnes.[3][4]
  • In Quanzhou, textiles value added grew 19.3%, among the fastest-growing sectors in the city.[5]
  • Fujian province made 2.084 billion metres of cloth (+5.7%) and 7.8863 million tonnes of chemical fibre (−4.0%).[6]

Flat national output and rising fibre volumes mean more competition for the same buyers. Mills that can show what is different about their fabric (a finish, a certification, a test result, a short sampling time) give buyers a reason to contact them rather than the next mill in the same city.

What overseas buyers compare in textiles and technical fabrics

Textile buyers compare suppliers within narrow product groups, and they search in the same terms:

Woven and knitted fabrics

Composition, weight in gsm, construction, width, colour fastness and MOQ per colour.

Technical and industrial textiles

Filtration media, reinforcement fabrics, coated and laminated textiles: performance data against the buyer’s application.

Nonwovens

Spunbond, meltblown and needle-punched materials for hygiene, medical, filtration and geotextile uses, with test results per grade.

Protective and functional fabrics

Flame-resistant, waterproof, antistatic or moisture-management fabrics, where buyers need the test standard and result for each claim.

Yarns and fibres

Recycled polyester, bio-based fibres and speciality yarns: traceability and certification of recycled content.

Textile machinery and finishing

Dyeing, printing, finishing and embroidery machines, compared on throughput, energy and water use and service.

Standards, rules and documents buyers ask about

Textile compliance is product- and market-specific. These are the references that come up most often with buyers in Europe and North America.

  • OEKO-TEX® STANDARD 100 certifies textiles tested for harmful substances; every thread, button and accessory is tested against a list of over 1,000 substances, with four product classes from baby articles to decoration materials.[7]
  • OEKO-TEX says its certification covers requirements including Annexes XVII and XIV of REACH, CPSIA and the ECHA candidate list of substances of very high concern.[7]
  • The EU’s Textile Labelling Regulation (EU) 1007/2011 requires textile products to be labelled or marked with their fibre composition whenever they are made available on the market; it does not cover size, origin or care labelling.[8]
  • REACH (EC 1907/2006) is the main EU law on chemical risks; it places responsibility on industry to manage the risks of the substances it uses.[9]
  • The EU’s Digital Product Passport will be introduced product group by product group; the Commission’s timeline lists textiles for an ecodesign delegated act in 2027.[10]

Write certificate claims exactly: which standard, which product class, which articles, certificate number and validity. Buyers can check OEKO-TEX labels online, so vague claims cost trust quickly.

Questions overseas buyers ask textiles and technical fabrics suppliers

What buyers ask first, and what your website can show so the answer is already there.

What is the exact composition and weight?

Put composition, gsm, width and construction on every fabric page, not only on the swatch card.

Which tests has this fabric passed?

List test standards and results per article, such as colour fastness, pilling, flame resistance or filtration efficiency.

What is your MOQ and how fast can you sample?

Minimum order per colour and per article, sample lead time and cost.

Can you prove recycled or organic content?

Say which certificates cover which articles and how traceability works.

Can you develop a custom fabric?

Explain your development process, lab capability and how long a new article typically takes.

How do you ship and to where?

Roll packing, typical container loads, ports and Incoterms you work with.

A website, SEO and RFQ playbook for textiles and technical fabrics manufacturers

Fabric sites often show beautiful photos and little data. Buyers need both. This is the order we usually work in for a mill or technical-textile maker.

  1. Build article pages for your main fabrics with composition, weight, width, finish and test results in text, plus a downloadable spec sheet.
  2. Group articles by end use (apparel, workwear, home, filtration, automotive, medical) so buyers who search by application find the right range.
  3. Publish a compliance page that lists certificates with scope, certificate numbers and validity, linked from each article.
  4. Show your lab and testing capability with the tests you run in house and the external labs you use.
  5. Write pages for specification searches, such as a named finish, a test standard or a fibre blend, rather than “fabric supplier China”.
  6. Offer a sample request form that captures article, colour, quantity and end use, and say how quickly you reply.
  7. Measure which articles and end-use pages produce sample requests, and expand those ranges online first.

Questions

Questions from textiles and technical fabrics manufacturers

How do we stand out when our city has hundreds of mills?

By publishing what is specific to you: your strongest articles, test results, finishes, sampling speed and certificates. Generic “high quality fabric” pages do not separate you from neighbours.

Should every fabric article have its own page?

Your main articles should. Smaller variants can sit on a range page with a table, as long as the key data is in text.

Can we show prices online?

Many mills show price bands or MOQ tiers rather than fixed prices. The important thing is that buyers know whether you fit their volume.

How should we present OEKO-TEX or other certificates?

Name the standard, product class, covered articles, certificate number and validity, and link to the official label check where one exists.

Do technical-textile buyers search differently from fashion buyers?

Yes. They search by application and performance (filtration efficiency, flame resistance, tensile strength), so those pages need data rather than mood photos.

Will you guarantee orders from Europe?

No. We make your mill easier to find and evaluate and report results honestly. Orders depend on your products, prices and service.

Is Hurfi located in Shaoxing?

No. Hurfi works from Xi’an and Bogura and supports mills anywhere in China remotely.
